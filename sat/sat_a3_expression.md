# Digital SAT Reading & Writing - Expression of Ideas (Domain A3)

---

## Scope

~20% of Reading and Writing (8-12 of 54 questions). Two skills only: **Rhetorical Synthesis** (choose the sentence that does the stated job using the notes) and **Transitions** (choose the word that shows how two ideas connect). The most mechanical and trainable domain on the test: fixed templates, no passage marathon. Points are lost by reading the notes before the goal, or picking what sounds good instead of what does the exact job.

---

## A3.1 Rhetorical Synthesis

---

### Definition in Simplest Terms

You are given a stack of research notes in bullet form (3 to 6 facts about one topic). Then you are told a specific job, like "emphasize a similarity between two projects" or "introduce a scientist and her field of study." Your task: pick the ONE sentence that does exactly that job using only facts from the notes.

Think of it as cooking from a recipe. The bullets are your ingredients. The goal is the recipe. Only one of the four dishes matches the recipe; the other three are perfectly edible food that you did not order. A choice can be 100% true and still be wrong, because the question is not "which sentence is true?" - it is "which sentence does THIS job?"

This question type is unique to the digital SAT. It did not exist on the paper-and-pencil SAT. It shows up on every single test, so it is worth mastering completely. The good news: the skill is entirely mechanical. Read the goal first, filter the notes, apply two elimination passes, verify. There is no way to be "surprised" by a rhetorical synthesis question once you have internalized the method.

---

**The core habit: read the goal before the notes.** The notes contain bait facts; the goal tells you which 2-3 facts matter and what shape the answer must take.

---

### Method (Fixed Order)

**Step 1: Read the goal first.** Identify the rhetorical purpose before looking at the notes. Underline the operative word: *emphasize, compare, contrast, summarize, explain, introduce, support, challenge.* Also note whether the goal names an audience ("for an audience unfamiliar with the topic" is a common add-on that changes everything).

**Step 2: Simplify the goal in your own words.** "They want me to show how X is different from Y." "They want me to name the scientist and say what she studies." This prevents the notes from pulling you off track. If the goal contains "and" (e.g., "introduce the scientist AND her field of study"), both halves are mandatory - a choice that does one half only is wrong.

**Step 3: Scan notes for goal-relevant facts.** Ignore details that don't serve the goal. You may need facts from 1 bullet point or across 4. Treat irrelevant bullets as distractor material. Mentally mark the 2 or 3 bullets the goal actually needs.

**Step 4: Evaluate each choice by asking two questions:**

| Question | If No: |
|---|---|
| Does this accomplish the stated goal? | Eliminate |
| Does this accurately reflect the notes? | Eliminate |

This is the two-pass elimination method (the same structure Khan Academy teaches for this question type):
- **Pass 1 (goal filter):** eliminate every choice that does not do the exact job. Be strict. If the goal is "introduce the scientist AND her field," a choice that names the scientist but not her field fails Pass 1 even though it is accurate.
- **Pass 2 (accuracy filter):** among the survivors, eliminate any choice that misstates, exaggerates, or adds information not in the notes. Watch for words like "exclusively," "only," "all," "proved," "always" - the notes rarely support absolutes.

**Step 5: Re-read the goal just before selecting.** Confirm your choice serves the exact stated purpose, not a different one that happens to be accurate. Then do a final sanity check: read your chosen sentence aloud in your head. Does it read naturally as a real English sentence? The correct answer always does; some distractors are grammatically awkward in ways that signal they were assembled to hit facts rather than to sound like writing.

---

### Goal Types

| Goal Type | What the Correct Answer Must Do | Keywords in Prompt |
|---|---|---|
| **Summarize** | Capture the breadth of the notes; cover multiple main points | summarize, provide an overview, describe the research on |
| **Emphasize / Highlight** | Foreground one specific aspect or finding | emphasize, highlight, stress, underscore |
| **Emphasize a similarity** | Name BOTH subjects and the trait they share, in one sentence | emphasize a similarity, both, in common |
| **Emphasize a difference / contrast** | Name BOTH subjects and what distinguishes them, with contrast structure | emphasize a difference, contrast, whereas, unlike |
| **Compare / Contrast** | Address two subjects explicitly using comparative/contrastive structure | compare, contrast, distinguish between, differentiate, similarities, differences |
| **Explain / Describe** | Clarify a process, cause, or mechanism | explain, describe, illustrate, show how |
| **Explain a concept + give an example** | Define the concept AND give a concrete instance from the notes | explain what X is and provide an example |
| **Present a study and its findings** | Identify the research AND report what it discovered | present a study and its findings |
| **Introduce** | Present the topic broadly to an audience (may specify "unfamiliar audience") | introduce, present to an audience unfamiliar with |
| **Support a claim** | Present evidence that backs a stated position | support, provide evidence for, corroborate |
| **Challenge a claim** | Present evidence that undermines a stated position | challenge, undermine, weaken, call into question |
| **List all members of a category** | Include EVERY item the goal names - none may be dropped | list, mention all, each of the |
| **Identify a trend** | Show a pattern across data or time | identify a trend, show how X has changed over time |
| **Describe a process in order** | Present the steps in the correct sequence | describe the process, in order, steps |

### Official College Board Sample Question

This is one of the two official sample questions College Board publishes for the Expression of Ideas domain (Assessment Framework v3.01, section 3.5.3.1). It is worth studying closely because the answer explanations are College Board's own.

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:
- Maika'i Tubbs is a Native Hawaiian sculptor and installation artist.
- His work has been shown in the United States, Canada, Japan, and Germany, among other places.
- Many of his sculptures feature discarded objects.
- His work Erasure (2008) includes discarded audiocassette tapes and magnets.
- His work Home Grown (2009) includes discarded pushpins, plastic plates and forks, and wood.

The student wants to emphasize a similarity between the two works. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

A) Erasure (2008) uses discarded objects such as audiocassette tapes and magnets; Home Grown (2009), however, includes pushpins, plastic plates and forks, and wood.
B) Like many of Tubbs's sculptures, both Erasure and Home Grown include discarded objects: Erasure uses audiocassette tapes, and Home Grown uses plastic forks.
C) Tubbs's work, which often features discarded objects, has been shown both within the United States and abroad.
D) Tubbs completed Erasure in 2008 and Home Grown in 2009.

**Correct answer: B** (College Board's key)

**Why:** The goal is to emphasize a SIMILARITY between the two works. Choice B uses "like many of Tubbs's sculptures" and "both" to emphasize that the two works share the use of discarded objects, even though the specific objects differ. It names both works, names the shared trait, and uses additive language ("both," "and") rather than contrast language.

**A is wrong:** **Trap 6 - Wrong relationship.** The use of "however" emphasizes a CONTRAST between the works (different discarded objects), but the goal asks for a similarity. The facts are accurate; the angle is backwards.

**C is wrong:** **Trap 3 - Wrong scope.** It discusses Tubbs's work in general and does not mention the two specific works at all. The goal is about a similarity between Erasure and Home Grown specifically.

**D is wrong:** **Trap 1 - Correct information, wrong goal.** It conveys completion dates for both works but establishes no logical relationship between them - no similarity, no shared trait. It does not do the job the goal demands.

#### [Easy] Summary Goal

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

- The Amazon rainforest spans approximately 5.5 million square kilometers across nine South American countries.
- It produces about 20% of the world's oxygen and is often called the "lungs of the Earth."
- Deforestation has reduced the forest by nearly 20% in the last fifty years.
- The Amazon is home to an estimated 10% of all known species on Earth.

The student wants to summarize key facts about the Amazon rainforest. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

A) The Amazon rainforest, which produces 20% of the world's oxygen, is home to 10% of all known species and spans 5.5 million square kilometers across nine countries, though deforestation has reduced it by nearly 20%.

B) The Amazon rainforest produces about 20% of the world's oxygen and is often called the "lungs of the Earth."

C) Deforestation has reduced the Amazon rainforest by nearly 20% in the last fifty years, threatening its biodiversity.

D) The Amazon rainforest spans approximately 5.5 million square kilometers, making it the largest tropical rainforest in the world.

---

**Correct answer: A**

**Why:** Choice A covers four major facts from the notes (size, oxygen production, biodiversity, deforestation), providing breadth appropriate for a summary.

**B is wrong:** **Trap 1 - Correct information, wrong goal (too narrow).** Only covers oxygen production, ignoring size, biodiversity, and deforestation. A summary must be comprehensive.

**C is wrong:** **Trap 3 - Wrong scope (too narrow for summary).** Focuses exclusively on deforestation. This might work for an "emphasize a threat" goal, but not for a general summary.

**D is wrong:** **Trap 1 - Correct information, wrong goal (too narrow).** Only covers size. Additionally, introduces "largest in the world" which is not stated in the notes - also **Trap 2 (contradicts notes)**.

#### [Easy] Introduce a Scientist and Her Field

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

- Dr. Aisha Bello is a marine biologist who studies coral reef ecosystems.
- She has published over 60 peer-reviewed papers on reef resilience.
- Her research has documented the recovery of coral reefs in the Gulf of Guinea after bleaching events.
- She directs a field station in Ghana where students learn reef monitoring techniques.

The student wants to introduce Dr. Bello and her field of study to an audience unfamiliar with the topic. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

A) Dr. Aisha Bello, a marine biologist who studies coral reef ecosystems, has published over 60 peer-reviewed papers and directs a field station in Ghana.
B) Dr. Aisha Bello has documented the recovery of coral reefs in the Gulf of Guinea after bleaching events.
C) Coral reef ecosystems are among the most biodiverse habitats on Earth, and Dr. Aisha Bello studies them.
D) Dr. Aisha Bello directs a field station in Ghana where students learn reef monitoring techniques.

---

**Correct answer: A**

**Why:** The goal is an "AND" goal: introduce the scientist AND her field. Choice A does both halves - it names her, identifies her as a marine biologist studying coral reefs (the field), and adds supporting facts about her work. It is also self-contained for an unfamiliar audience: nothing is assumed.

**B is wrong:** **Trap 7 - The "and" goal, only half done.** It introduces the scientist but never names her field (marine biology, coral reefs are implied by the content but the field itself is not stated). An unfamiliar audience learns what she did, not what field she works in.

**C is wrong:** **Trap 3 - Wrong scope (too broad for introduce).** It spends most of the sentence on a general fact about coral reefs ("among the most biodiverse habitats on Earth") that is NOT in the notes - **Trap 2** - and introduces Dr. Bello only as an afterthought.

**D is wrong:** **Trap 7 - Half done + narrow.** It names the scientist and one activity (directing a field station) but never says what field she is in. For an unfamiliar audience, this fails the "introduce the field" half of the goal.

#### [Medium] Explain a Process

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

- CRISPR-Cas9 is a gene-editing technology derived from a bacterial immune system.
- The Cas9 enzyme acts as "molecular scissors" that cut DNA at a specific location.
- A guide RNA molecule is designed to match the target DNA sequence and directs Cas9 to the correct spot.
- Once the DNA is cut, the cell's natural repair mechanisms can be used to disable a gene or insert a new sequence.

The student wants to explain how CRISPR-Cas9 edits genes. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

A) CRISPR-Cas9 is a gene-editing technology that has revolutionized genetic research and holds promise for treating genetic disorders.

B) CRISPR-Cas9 uses a guide RNA to direct the Cas9 enzyme to a specific DNA location, where Cas9 cuts the DNA; the cell's repair mechanisms can then disable the gene or insert a new sequence.

C) The Cas9 enzyme acts as "molecular scissors" that cut DNA at a specific location, and the technology is derived from a bacterial immune system.

D) Derived from a bacterial immune system, CRISPR-Cas9 is a powerful tool for editing genes by cutting DNA and allowing cells to repair themselves.

---

**Correct answer: B**

**Why:** Choice B presents the process in chronological order: guide RNA directs Cas9, Cas9 cuts, cell repair mechanisms act. This is the minimum set of steps needed to explain the mechanism.

**A is wrong:** **Trap 1 - Correct information, wrong goal.** Discusses the significance of CRISPR rather than the mechanism. Contains no process steps.

**C is wrong:** **Trap 3 - Wrong scope (too narrow).** Only covers the cutting step but omits the guide RNA and repair mechanism, which are essential to explaining how gene editing works.

**D is wrong:** **Trap 2 - Goal partially achieved but vague.** Mentions cutting and repair but omits the crucial guide RNA step that makes the process specific. Also uses vague language ("powerful tool") that does not explain the mechanism.

#### [Hard] Support a Claim

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

- Urban green spaces include parks, community gardens, and green roofs.
- A 2021 study in Toronto found that neighborhoods with tree canopy coverage above 30% had 12% lower rates of emergency mental health calls.
- A 2022 study in London showed that office workers with views of green spaces reported 15% higher job satisfaction.
- Access to green spaces varies by income; low-income neighborhoods in US cities have 40% less park access than affluent ones.

The student wants to support the claim that access to urban green spaces improves mental well-being. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

A) Urban green spaces include parks, community gardens, and green roofs, though access to them varies significantly by income level.

B) A 2021 Toronto study found 12% lower emergency mental health calls in high-canopy neighborhoods, and a 2022 London study found 15% higher job satisfaction among workers with green views, suggesting that green space access benefits mental well-being.

C) Low-income neighborhoods in US cities have 40% less park access than affluent ones, which is an environmental justice issue.

D) Office workers with views of green spaces reported 15% higher job satisfaction in a 2022 London study, and urban green spaces include parks, community gardens, and green roofs.

---

**Correct answer: B**

**Why:** Choice B directly links two pieces of evidence (Toronto study, London study) to the claim about mental well-being, using the language of support ("suggesting that... benefits mental well-being"). Evidence + claim, in one sentence - the exact shape a support goal requires.

**A is wrong:** **Trap 1 - Correct information, wrong goal.** The access-inequality fact challenges rather than supports the claim about mental well-being. (Also usable for a "challenge" goal but not this one.)

**C is wrong:** **Trap 1 - Correct information, wrong goal.** Focuses on inequality, not on supporting the mental well-being claim. This would work for an "identify a disparity" goal.

**D is wrong:** **Trap 5 - Includes irrelevant fact.** The London study is relevant, but the definition of green spaces ("parks, community gardens, and green roofs") does not support the mental well-being claim. The choice fails to connect the evidence to the claim.

#### [Very Hard] Support a Claim with Two Required Elements

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

- A team at Stanford University studied the effect of daily meditation on attention in 120 office workers.
- Participants meditated 20 minutes per day for eight weeks.
- After eight weeks, participants scored 14% higher on sustained-attention tasks.
- The researchers also measured sleep quality and found modest improvements.
- The study's authors caution that the results show correlation, not causation.

The student wants to support the claim that daily meditation improves attention, citing both the study's duration and its outcome. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

A) A Stanford University study found that 120 office workers who meditated 20 minutes daily for eight weeks scored 14% higher on sustained-attention tasks afterward, supporting the claim that daily meditation improves attention.
B) After eight weeks of daily meditation, participants in a Stanford study scored 14% higher on sustained-attention tasks and reported modest improvements in sleep quality.
C) The Stanford study's authors caution that the results show correlation, not causation, so the claim that meditation improves attention remains unproven.
D) Daily meditation has been shown to improve attention: in a Stanford study, 120 office workers who meditated scored higher on attention tasks.

---

**Correct answer: A**

**Why:** This is an "AND" goal: the student must cite BOTH the study's duration AND its outcome while supporting the claim. Choice A delivers the duration (20 minutes daily for eight weeks), the outcome (14% higher on sustained-attention tasks), and the claim ("supporting the claim that daily meditation improves attention"). All three elements present.

**B is wrong:** **Trap 7 - Half done.** It gives the outcome and duration but the sleep-quality finding is irrelevant to the attention claim - and, more importantly, the choice never connects the evidence to the claim. There is no "supporting the claim" language and no stated claim. It reports data without completing the rhetorical job.

**C is wrong:** **Trap 1 - Correct information, wrong goal.** It uses the caution note to UNDERMINE the claim rather than support it. This would serve a "challenge" goal. The facts are accurate; the direction is backwards (Trap 8 - same facts, wrong perspective).

**D is wrong:** **Trap 7 - Half done + vague.** It names the study and the outcome but drops the specific duration (20 minutes daily for eight weeks) that the goal explicitly requires. "Meditated" without the duration fails the "AND" test. It also asserts "has been shown to improve attention" more strongly than the notes support - the authors caution the results are correlational, so "shown" overstates (Trap 2 - overreach).

## A3.2 Transitions

---

### Definition in Simplest Terms

A short passage has a blank. On either side of the blank are two ideas. You must pick the transition word or phrase that best shows how those two ideas are related - whether the second idea opposes the first, follows from it, adds to it, illustrates it, concedes to it, or comes after it in time.

Every transition word makes a small logical promise. "Therefore" promises the next idea follows as a result. "For example" promises a specific instance of a broader point. "Nevertheless" acknowledges the previous point and says the next claim still holds. Your job is to figure out which promise the passage actually needs, then pick the word that makes exactly that promise.

Crucial fact: every answer choice is grammatically correct. You are never choosing between "correct grammar" and "wrong grammar." You are choosing between four words that all fit the sentence grammatically - only one fits the LOGIC. This is why you cannot answer by ear alone ("it sounds right") and why you must name the relationship before you look at the choices.

---

### The Method

**Step 1: Cover the answer choices.** Reading them first creates confirmation bias. If you see "however" in the choices, your brain starts hunting for a contrast even when the passage is additive. Cover them.

**Step 2: Read the sentence before the blank and the sentence after. Paraphrase each in your head.** Translate each into a simple bullet point. "Sentence 1 says the project cost more than planned. Sentence 2 says it cut commute times." If the passage is hard to parse, simplify each sentence until you can state its claim in a few words.

**Step 3: Identify the precise logical relationship.** Do not use vague labels like "it connects them." Ask:

| Question | Likely Relationship |
|---|---|
| Does the second sentence follow from the first? | Cause-Effect |
| Does the second sentence oppose or reverse the first? | Contrast |
| Does the second sentence acknowledge the first but pivot? | Concession |
| Does the second sentence add a similar point? | Addition |
| Does the second sentence illustrate the first? | Example |
| Does the second sentence happen after the first? | Sequence |
| Does the second sentence emphasize or clarify the first? | Emphasis |
| Does the second sentence present an alternative? | Result/Exception |

**Step 4: Predict your own transition word.** Use a simple, concrete word that matches the relationship. *"This is contrast, so I need something like 'however'."* Say it in your own words first: "the second sentence pushes back against the first." THEN match to a category.

**Step 5: Uncover the choices and match.** Do not read every word - scan for the category you predicted first. Only compare within-category if multiple choices match your relationship label.

**Step 6: Substitute and sanity-check.** Plug your pick into the blank and read the full sentence in your head. Does the logic flow? If the passage reads like a real paragraph, you are done.

Two extra tips from Khan Academy's SAT course:

- **Be flexible.** The exact word you predicted ("therefore") may not be among the choices. Look for a word that does the same job. A cause-effect relationship can be served by "therefore," "as a result," "consequently," "accordingly," or "thus." If your predicted word is absent, look for a synonym in the same family, not a word from a different family.

Also remember PrepMaven's warning: **if two answers from the same category both seem right, go back to the passage.** There is never more than one correct answer, so if your relationship label admits two choices, your label is probably too vague. Tighten it: it is not just "contrast" - it is "contrast that survives a conceded obstacle" (nevertheless) or "direct opposition" (however) or "inverted relationship" (conversely).

### Official College Board Sample Question

This is the second official sample question College Board publishes for this domain (Assessment Framework v3.01, section 3.5.3.1). The explanation below follows College Board's own key explanation.

Iraqi artist Nazik Al-Malaika, celebrated as the first Arabic poet to write in free verse, didn't reject traditional forms entirely; her poem "Elegy for a Woman of No Importance" consists of two ten-line stanzas and a standard number of syllables. Even in this superficially traditional work, ______ Al-Malaika was breaking new ground by memorializing an anonymous woman rather than a famous man.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

A) in fact,
B) though,
C) therefore,
D) moreover,

**Correct answer: B** (College Board's key)

**Why:** The first sentence establishes that although Al-Malaika is famous for free verse, she still used traditional forms in "Elegy." The last sentence qualifies that point: even in this traditional work, she was "breaking new ground" by choosing an anonymous woman as her subject. The two ideas disagree - traditional form vs. new ground. "Though" (along with "even" earlier in the sentence) signals this contrast/qualification: she subverted tradition even when using traditional forms. The relationship is contrast - a qualification of the point just made.

**A is wrong:** "In fact" would signal that the last sentence STRENGTHENS or amplifies the truth of the first. But the last sentence qualifies and partially opposes it, not amplifies it. **Trap: Emphasis vs. Contrast.**

**C is wrong:** "Therefore" would signal that the last sentence is a CONSEQUENCE of the first. But breaking new ground is not caused by her use of traditional forms - it stands in tension with it. **Trap: Cause-Effect vs. Contrast.**

**D is wrong:** "Moreover" would signal that the last sentence merely ADDS more information in the same direction. But it does not add to the point about traditional forms; it qualifies and complicates it. **Trap: Addition vs. Contrast.**

The lesson: all three wrong answers are grammatically perfect. They fail on logic alone. This is why you must name the relationship (contrast/qualification) before looking at the choices.

### Examples

#### [Easy] Addition

Urban green spaces provide recreational areas for residents. ________, they help reduce the urban heat island effect by cooling surrounding neighborhoods.

A) However
B) For example
C) In addition
D) Consequently

---

**Correct answer: C**

**Why:** Sentence 2 adds another benefit of urban green spaces. The relationship is addition.

**A is wrong:** **Trap 4 - "However" overused.** There is no opposition between the two benefits.

**B is wrong:** **Trap 3 - Addition vs. Example confusion.** Sentence 2 is not a specific instance of sentence 1; it is a separate benefit.

**D is wrong:** **Trap 2 - Cause-Effect vs. Sequence confusion.** While green spaces do cool neighborhoods, sentence 2 is not presented as a consequence of sentence 1's claim about recreation. The relationship is additive, not causal.

#### [Easy] Example / Illustration

Some plant species delay blooming until environmental conditions are favorable. ________, the alpine dryad, a small Arctic flower, waits for the snow to melt before producing its blossoms.

A) For instance,
B) However,
C) Therefore,
D) Moreover,

---

**Correct answer: A**

**Why:** Sentence 1 makes a general claim about a category (plants that delay blooming). Sentence 2 names one specific member of that category (the alpine dryad). That is the definition of exemplification: a concrete instance of the broader statement.

**B is wrong:** **Trap 4 - "However" overused.** There is no opposition. The dryad is a case that CONFIRMS the general claim, not one that contradicts it.

**C is wrong:** **Trap 2 - Cause-Effect vs. Example confusion.** The dryad's behavior is not caused by sentence 1's claim; it is an illustration of it.

**D is wrong:** **Trap 3 - Addition vs. Example confusion.** Sentence 2 is not a separate new point; it is a specific example of the point just made. When the second sentence fits INSIDE the first sentence's category, the relationship is example, not addition.

#### [Medium] Emphasis

The proposal appeared to offer only modest benefits. ________, independent analysts calculated that it would save the city $40 million over five years.

A) In fact,
B) However,
C) Consequently,
D) For instance,

---

**Correct answer: A**

**Why:** Sentence 2 verifies and INTENSIFIES the situation set up in sentence 1 - the proposal that "appeared" modest actually saves $40 million. "In fact" is the classic transition for presenting a stronger truth that corrects or sharpens the impression just given.

**B is wrong:** **Trap 4 - "However" overused.** Sentence 2 does not oppose sentence 1; it refines it. The appearance of modesty is corrected by the real number - that is emphasis, not contrast.

**C is wrong:** **Trap 2 - Cause-Effect vs. Emphasis confusion.** The $40 million figure is not caused by the appearance of modesty; it is the actual magnitude beneath that appearance.

**D is wrong:** **Trap 3 - Example vs. Emphasis confusion.** The $40 million is not an example of the category "modest benefits"; it contradicts that impression and reveals the true scale.

#### [Medium] Cause-Effect

The volcanic eruption of Mount Tambora in 1815 released vast amounts of sulfur dioxide into the stratosphere. ________, global temperatures dropped by as much as 0.7 degrees Celsius the following year, causing widespread crop failures.

A) Nevertheless
B) For example
C) As a result
D) Meanwhile

---

**Correct answer: C**

**Why:** The eruption (cause) directly produced a temperature drop (effect). This is cause-effect.

**A is wrong:** **Trap 1 - Contrast/Concession confusion.** There is no opposition. The temperature drop is an expected consequence, not a counterpoint.

**B is wrong:** **Trap 3 - Example vs. Cause-Effect confusion.** Sentence 2 is not an example of the eruption; it is its consequence.

**D is wrong:** **Trap 7 - Meanwhile vs. However confusion.** "Meanwhile" signals simultaneity, but the temperature drop happened the following year, not at the same time.

---

#### [Hard] Contrast vs. Concession

Many critics argue that social media has weakened genuine interpersonal connections by replacing face-to-face interaction with superficial online engagement. ________, some studies suggest that social media can strengthen existing relationships by facilitating frequent communication across distances.

A) Granted
B) Consequently
C) In fact
D) Nevertheless

---

**Correct answer: D**

**Why:** Sentence 1 presents a critical position. Sentence 2 presents a countervailing finding that contradicts the implication that social media only weakens connections. This is contrast, not concession, because sentence 1 is not being "granted" as valid before pushing back - it is being directly opposed. "Nevertheless" signals "despite what critics say, this other finding holds."

**A is wrong:** **Trap 1 - Concession vs. Contrast confusion.** If the passage began "Granted, social media has drawbacks," then pivoted, that would be concession. But here, sentence 1 presents the critics' position as a claim, and sentence 2 presents a finding that contravenes it. The passage does not first admit the critics are right.

**B is wrong:** **Trap 2 - Cause-Effect vs. Contrast confusion.** Sentence 2 is not a consequence of the critics' argument.

**C is wrong:** **Trap 3 - Emphasis vs. Contrast confusion.** "In fact" would strengthen or clarify sentence 1, but sentence 2 pushes against sentence 1.

#### [Very Hard] Cause-Effect Direction Reversal

The eastern monarch butterfly population has declined by over 80% in the last two decades. ________, the loss of milkweed plants - the only food source for monarch caterpillars - due to herbicide use has reduced their reproductive success.

A) For instance,
B) However,
C) This decline is largely because
D) As a result,

---

**Correct answer: C**

**Why:** This question reverses the typical cause-effect order. Sentence 1 states the effect (population decline). Sentence 2 states the cause (milkweed loss). "This decline is largely because" correctly signals that the second sentence explains the first.

**A is wrong:** **Trap 3 - Example vs. Explanation confusion.** Sentence 2 is not an example of the decline; it is the reason for it.

**B is wrong:** **Trap 1 - Contrast vs. Explanation confusion.** There is no opposition. Sentence 2 explains sentence 1.

**D is wrong:** **Trap 2 - Cause-Effect direction confusion.** "As a result" would make sentence 2 the effect of sentence 1, which reverses the actual causal direction. This is the classic cause-effect direction trap.

## Rhetorical Effectiveness (Concision, Style, Precision)

Not a separate testing point, but 1-2 questions per test hide inside this domain: every choice is grammatical and the best-written one wins. Three filters, in order: (1) kill redundancy, (2) kill off-register words, (3) of the survivors, pick the most precise. Shortest wins only if meaning and tone survive intact.

### Concision

The shortest grammatical option preserving all meaning is usually right. **Deletion test:** if a phrase can go with zero meaning lost, cut it.

| Redundant | Concise |
|---|---|
| the reason is because | because |
| in spite of the fact that / due to the fact that | although / because |
| in order to | to |
| has the ability to / is able to | can |
| consensus agreement / free gift / new innovation | consensus / gift / innovation |
| collaborate together / repeat again / return back | collaborate / repeat / return |
| at this point in time / on a daily basis | now / daily |
| it is important to note that | (delete) |

### Style and Tone

Match the passage's register: academic passages need formal words. Signal check: vocabulary like "considerable," "yielded," "consequently" means formal, so "totally psyched" in that neighborhood is always wrong.

### Precision

The word's strength must match the evidence. A study that "found" a correlation plus a choice saying it "proved" causation is wrong. Researchers who "suspect" a mechanism plus a choice saying they "demonstrated" it is wrong. Too weak is equally imprecise. Common pairs: "suggest" vs "prove," "debate" vs "argue about," "declined" vs "went down"; "consistent with" never becomes "proves." Verify numbers and qualifiers survive the trim: "nearly 80%" is not "80%".

---

#### [Very Hard] Precision vs. Overstatement

According to the study's authors, the results ________ that the new drug reduces inflammation, though they caution that larger trials are needed to confirm the effect.

A) prove
B) suggest
C) guarantee
D) establish beyond doubt

---

**Correct answer: B**

**Why:** The sentence itself contains the caution: "larger trials are needed to confirm the effect." That caution means the results do not yet prove anything - they only point toward an effect. "Suggest" matches the evidence's strength exactly.

**A is wrong:** **Overstatement.** "Prove" contradicts the authors' own caution. If the results proved the effect, larger trials would not be needed to "confirm" it. The sentence would become self-contradictory.

**C is wrong:** **Overstatement (stronger).** "Guarantee" is even stronger than "prove" - the drug's effect cannot be guaranteed by one study's results.

**D is wrong:** **Overstatement (wordy).** "Establish beyond doubt" is a wordy way of saying "prove," and it overstates for the same reason. This choice shows how overstatement and wordiness often travel together in the wrong answers.

## Complete Transition Taxonomy Table

| Category | Function | Key Transitions |
|---|---|---|
| Sequence | Order in time or steps | then, subsequently, first/second/third, next, afterward, later, previously, finally, meanwhile, eventually |
| Contrast | Direct opposition | however, conversely, in contrast, on the other hand, nevertheless, nonetheless, yet, whereas, by contrast, still, even so |
| Concession | Admit point then counter | granted, admittedly, of course, certainly, to be sure, despite this, although |
| Cause-Effect | One produces the other | therefore, consequently, as a result, thus, hence, accordingly, because, since, in turn |
| Addition | Extend or reinforce | furthermore, moreover, in addition, additionally, also, besides, indeed, similarly, likewise |
| Emphasis | Strengthen or clarify | indeed, in fact, notably, particularly, specifically, above all, especially |
| Example | Illustrate general point | for example, for instance, specifically, in particular, namely, to illustrate |
| Result/Exception | Logical outcome or alternative | as a result, therefore, otherwise, instead, rather, alternatively |

**The two families that overlap across categories:** "indeed" appears in both Addition and Emphasis (it adds AND strengthens), and "as a result"/"therefore" appear in both Cause-Effect and Result/Exception. When a word appears in two rows, the passage's exact relationship decides which job it is doing. "Indeed" after a claim that it confirms = emphasis; "indeed" adding a further confirming fact = addition. Both are "same direction" moves, so they rarely create wrong-answer confusion - the important split is same-direction (addition/emphasis/example) vs. direction-change (contrast/concession).

## One-Page Cheat Sheet

### Rhetorical Synthesis
- **Read the goal FIRST.** Not the notes. The goal decides which facts matter and what shape the answer must take.
- **Two-question test:** Does this accomplish the goal? Does it accurately reflect the notes? If either answer is no, eliminate.
- **Be strict with "AND" goals.** If the goal demands two elements ("scientist AND her field," "duration AND outcome"), both must appear. Half-done = wrong.
- **Breadth for summary, depth for emphasis.** Know which goal type you have.
- **The goal verb predicts the shape.** Emphasize a difference = contrast structure with both subjects. Emphasize a similarity = "both" + shared trait. Introduce = name + define. Support = evidence + claim. List all = every member present.
- **Re-read the goal last.** Before selecting, confirm alignment.
- **Check it reads naturally.** The right answer always sounds like a real sentence.

### Transitions
- **Cover the choices.** Define the relationship before you see the options.
- **Paraphrase both sides, then name the relationship in your own words.** "Sentence 2 pushes back against sentence 1" = contrast. "Sentence 2 is caused by sentence 1" = cause-effect.
- **Predict your own word.** "This is contrast, so I need 'however'."
- **Be flexible.** Your exact word may be absent; pick a word from the same family.
- **Copycat clue (secondary only).** If two choices mean essentially the same thing, be suspicious, because one correct answer usually cannot distinguish between them. Never let this override the passage relationship - name the relationship first.
- **Contrast vs. Concession:** Concession admits the first point is valid before countering ("yes, but..."). Contrast simply opposes.
- **However vs. Nevertheless:** However = simple shift. Nevertheless = the point survives a conceded obstacle.
- **Cause-Effect vs. Sequence:** Temporal order is sequence. Causal relationship is cause-effect. "Later" does not mean "because."
- **Cause-Effect direction:** Effect stated first + cause second = "because"/"since." Cause first + effect second = "therefore"/"as a result."
- **Addition vs. Example:** Specific instance = example. New related point = addition.
- **Never pick "however" by default.** Verify actual contrast.

### Rhetorical Effectiveness
- **Shortest correct option wins** - unless it changes meaning or tone.
- **Run the deletion test.** If deleting a phrase loses nothing, delete it.
- **Match the passage's register.** Academic passages need formal language.
- **Choose precise words.** "Debate" not "argue about"; "decline" not "went down"; "suggest" not "prove" (unless the evidence proves).
- **Verify numbers and qualifiers survive the trim.** "Nearly 80%" is not "80%."

# SAT Reading and Writing Cheat Sheet

## Information and Ideas

- Identify the claim before evaluating evidence.
- Choose the answer directly supported by the text or data.
- For inference questions, select the smallest conclusion that must be true.

## Craft and Structure

- Define unfamiliar words using the sentence's context, not their most common meaning.
- For paired texts, compare the authors' claims and evidence—not just their topics.
- For purpose questions, ask what the quoted sentence does in the passage.

## Expression of Ideas

- Choose transitions by logical relationship: contrast, cause/effect, example, continuation, or conclusion.
- Rhetorical-synthesis questions require using the notes only for the stated goal.
- Remove repetition and prefer the most precise concise choice.

## Standard English Conventions

- A semicolon joins two complete sentences.
- A colon follows a complete sentence and introduces an explanation or list.
- A comma cannot join two complete sentences by itself.
- Keep subjects and verbs consistent in number.
- Place modifiers next to the words they describe.
- Keep items in a list grammatically parallel.
- Use apostrophes for possession, not ordinary plurals.
- Verb tense should match the timeline and surrounding verbs.

A brief guide to the reference information—AKA the formula sheet—available to test takers on the SAT Math section.

### Reference information available on the SAT Math section

The following reference sheet is provided for you on the Math section of the SAT: 

![Image 2: Reference figure 1 is a circle with radius r. Two equations are presented below reference figure 1. A equals pi times the square of r. ; C equals 2 pi r.

Reference figure 2 is a rectangle with length l and width w. An equation is presented below reference figure 2. A equals l w.

Reference figure 3 is a triangle with base b and height h. An equation is presented below reference figure 3. A equals one half b h.

Reference figure 4 is a right triangle. The two sides that form the right angle are labeled a and b, and the side opposite the right angle is labeled c. An equation is presented below reference figure 4. c squared equals a squared plus b squared.

Reference figure 5 is a right triangle with a 30 degree angle and a 60 degree angle. The side opposite the 30 degree angle is labeled x. The side opposite the 60 degree angle is labeled x times the square root of 3. The side opposite the right angle is labeled 2 x.

Reference figure 6 is a right triangle with two 45 degree angles. Two sides are each labeled s. The side opposite the right angle is labeled s times the square root of 2.

Reference figure 7 is a rectangular solid whose base has length l and width w and whose height is h. An equation is presented below reference figure 7. V equals l w h.

Reference figure 8 is a right circular cylinder whose base has radius r and whose height is h. An equation is presented below reference figure 8. V equals pi times the square of r times h.

Reference figure 9 is a sphere with radius r. An equation is presented below reference figure 9. V equals four thirds pi times the cube of r.

Reference figure 10 is a cone whose base has radius r and whose height is h. An equation is presented below reference figure 10. V equals one third times pi times the square of r times h.

Reference figure 11 is an asymmetrical pyramid whose base has length l and width w and whose height is h. An equation is presented below reference figure 11. V equals one third l w h.

In addition to the 11 figures, the image also contains the following three text sentences:
The number of degrees of arc in a circle is 360.
The number of radians of arc in a circle is 2 pi.
The sum of the measures in degrees of the angles of a triangle is 180.](https://cdn.kastatic.org/ka-perseus-graphie/dc7442008d96940c0b9bdaab490e6f3607b22b1b.svg)

$\text{The number of degrees of arc in a circle is}\textrm{ } 360.$‍$\text{The number of radians of arc in a circle is}\textrm{ } 2 \pi .$‍$\text{The sum of the measures in degrees of the angles of a triangle is}\textrm{ } 180.$‍$r$‍$A = \pi r^{2}$‍$C = 2 \pi r$‍$A = ℓ w$‍$ℓ$‍$w$‍$A = \frac{1}{2} b h$‍$h$‍$b$‍$c^{2} = a^{2} + b^{2}$‍$b$‍$a$‍$c$‍$\text{Special Right Triangles}$‍$2 x$‍$60 °$‍$30 °$‍$x \sqrt{3}$‍$x$‍$s$‍$45 °$‍$45 °$‍$s$‍$s \sqrt{2}$‍$ℓ$‍$w$‍$h$‍$V = ℓ w h$‍$V = \pi r^{2} h$‍$h$‍$r$‍$r$‍$V = \frac{4}{3} \pi r^{3}$‍$V = \frac{1}{3} \pi r^{2} h$‍$h$‍$ℓ$‍$w$‍$h$‍$r$‍$V = \frac{1}{3} ℓ w h$‍

Notice that the sheet includes formulas for solving circumference, the area of circles, triangles, and rectangles, and the volume of rectangular prisms, cylinders, spheres, cones, and pyramids. It also includes the Pythagorean Theorem and a breakdown of the angles in special right triangles.

In the Bluebook testing application, you can access the reference sheet by clicking on "reference" in the upper right-hand corner.

While this information is there if you need it, **it's important to know these formulas and concepts before you take the test.** We cover them in the Geometry and Trigonometry units in this course—along with other formulas and equations that _aren't_ available to you on the reference sheet, but which you'll still need to know for some Geometry and Trigonometry questions. 

 Happy prepping!

[HeyItsAshy](https://www.khanacademy.org/profile/kaid_904919017162081037210174/discussion) [a month ago Posted a month ago. Direct link to HeyItsAshy's post “No, they unfortunately do...”](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/v2-sat-math/x0fcc98a58ba3bea7:about-the-digital-sat/x0fcc98a58ba3bea7:preparing-for-the-sat/a/reference-information-on-the-digital-sat-math-test?qa_expand_key=ag5zfmtoYW4tYWNhZGVteXJACxIIVXNlckRhdGEiHWthaWRfOTA0OTE5MDE3MTYyMDgxMDM3MjEwMTc0DAsSCEZlZWRiYWNrGICA17TqvIQIDA&qa_expand_type=answer)   more   No, they unfortunately do not. The official formula sheet they give you on the SAT is actually pretty disappointing. They only include basic geometry and trigonometry stuff, like the areas of triangles and circles, the Pythagorean theorem, and the formulas for the volume of 3D shapes like cones and spheres.

That means coordinate geometry formulas—like slope, distance, and the midpoint formula—are completely left off the reference sheet. You have to memorize those yourself before test day.

There is a massive shortcut for this though. Since you have Desmos built right into the digital SAT, you don't always need the distance or midpoint formulas if you get stuck. If you have two points, you can just plot them on the Desmos graph and visually find the middle of the line, or use the Pythagorean theorem on the grid to find the distance. But for slope, definitely make sure you have the classic "rise over run" formula memorized because the test loves to ask about linear equations in ways that require the actual math.

## 1. Experience related to your major (150 words max)

I built a stock market that does not exist. NEXUS is a simulated exchange with a real order-matching engine and nine kinds of traders: herders, momentum traders, attention-limited traders, market makers, and more. I built it to test my research on investor attention from a direction real data cannot offer, because you cannot rerun 2020 but you can rerun a simulated market ten thousand times.

The hard part was making the code fast and honest. Research needs thousands of runs, so I rewrote the matching engine with compiled kernels until orders cleared in about seven microseconds. Then I checked whether the fake market behaved like a real one. Calibrated runs reproduce the nine patterns I test for, including fat-tailed returns and volatility clustering.

It taught me what computer science means to me: turning a messy human system into rules, then finding where those rules fail.

## 2. Goals after graduating (150 words max)

I want to build systems that people can trust with real decisions, especially in finance, where models move money and confident mistakes are expensive. Three people now use ATLAS, my quant research platform, for their own work. After graduation, I want to work as a quantitative researcher or engineer, then build tools that show their reasoning, not just their outputs.

At Illinois, CS 442: Trustworthy Machine Learning is the course I most want, because it asks the question my projects keep running into: when should anyone believe a model? Through PURE, I want to join a faculty research team early, as a junior member learning how a lab tests its own results, which is the one thing I have never had working alone.

## 3. Second-choice major (150 words max) [write only if you select one]

Statistics & Computer Science is my second choice because half of my work is deciding whether a result is real. My research on investor attention across 21 markets produced a threshold so exact it looked suspicious. Testing it needed bootstrap methods that respect how markets move together, corrections across 23 hypotheses, and a holdout I froze before looking at new data. I enjoyed that work as much as building the code.

Statistics & CS would let me study the mathematics of evidence and the engineering of software together. Even if I end up there instead of CS, my goal stays the same: to build systems whose answers I can defend.

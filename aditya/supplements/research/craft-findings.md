# Research Findings for the Supplement Set

## What official admissions guidance changes

MIT repeatedly says that its short essays are snapshots rather than an achievement contest. Its admissions team values collaboration, hands-on action, thoughtful risk, community responsibility, and genuine play. That means the fun response should remain football or pickleball, and the challenge response should admit the control problem in Future Lab instead of converting it into a winning project story.

Stanford describes its questions as a way to meet a future friend, roommate, and classmate. The set therefore needs domestic and social details alongside intellectual work. The roommate note should keep the 10:30 bedtime, clean room, missing key, family calls, South Indian food, football, and tendency to overexplain.

Princeton separates academic interest, lived experience, service, and short personal answers. Reusing research across all four would defeat that design. The Black–Scholes club failure belongs in “Your Voice” because it shows how Aditya enters conversations; the technical content is only the source of the social mistake.

UCAS readers consider the three answers together. Question 1 establishes academic direction, Question 2 interprets formal preparation, and Question 3 explains why outside experiences matter. Three independent mini-essays with repeated introductions would waste characters.

## Lessons from official student examples

MIT student essays that age well tend to focus on a small activity or failure and do not pretend to change the planet. Their writers often dislike the lines that were engineered to be memorable. The useful lesson is restraint: choose the real object and let the response remain proportionate.

Strong academic statements move in this order: a bounded observation, the question it created, what the student did, what changed in the student’s understanding, and what formal study is still missing. Lists of method names or university resources cannot replace that movement.

## Set-level rules

1. No university should receive four versions of “I build technical projects.”
2. A research number appears only when the number changes the reader’s understanding of the question.
3. School resources earn space only when they supply something Aditya cannot create alone: a critic, partner, formal foundation, affected user, or institutional setting.
4. Do not make every ending wise. Some should stop on a habit, question, image, or limitation.
5. The activity list proves scale. Essays must prove perception.

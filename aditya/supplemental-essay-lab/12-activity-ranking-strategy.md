## 1. How AOs Actually Read the List (Top-Heavy Scan)

> **Slots 1–3 receive the most attention; slots 4–7 get a quicker scan; slots 8–10 are often background noise.** — [Ivy100 Education: Complete 2026 Guide](https://www.ivy100edu.ca/blog/common-app-activities-ordering), [GreatCollegeAdvice: Narrative Anchor](https://greatcollegeadvice.com/blog/how-to-fill-the-common-application-activities-section/)

> **"Your slot 1 activity sets the interpretive frame for everything that follows. If your #1 is a three-year research internship at UBC, every subsequent activity gets read through a 'serious STEM student' lens."** — Sara Harberson (former associate dean, Penn), via Ivy100

> **Activities #1–3: Core Identity. #4–6: Significant Supporting. #7–10: Additional Involvement / breadth.** — [AIM Evaluations: Ranking Your Activities](https://aimevaluations.com/blog-education-answers/ranking-your-activities-on-the-common-appnbsp)

**Implication for Aditya:** Do NOT rank chronologically or by hours/week. Rank by **narrative anchor** — the spike that explains the rest. If slots 1–3 don't establish "builder-researcher who tests systems vs just describing them," the list reads as scattered even if every entry is strong.

### 1.1 The 90-Second Test (AIM + GradGPT)

If an AO only reads your first three activities, can they answer:
1. What you care about?
2. How you spend your time?
3. What responsibilities you've taken on vs. participated in?

If not — reorder.

## 3. Ordering Rules (All Sources Converge)

### 3.1 Narrative Anchor Framework (GreatCollegeAdvice)

1. **Slot 1 — Anchor/Headline:** The single activity that best establishes your spike/core narrative. Not the most hours, but the most defining. For Aditya: GASI (investor-attention threshold) or NEXUS (simulated market corroboration) — either anchors "systems under uncertainty."

2. **Slots 2–4 — Reinforce the anchor from a different angle:**
   - If Slot 1 = research (GASI), Slot 2 = different *method* on same question (NEXUS SMM), Slot 3 = *building for others* (ATLAS or Glassbox), Slot 4 = *leadership infrastructure* (Quant Finance Club / Future Lab).
   - This is the **"After joining A, Abby started B, founded C, and brought teams to accomplish XYZ"** string that makes AOs slow down ([CollegeAdvisor](https://www.collegeadvisor.com/resources/common-app-activities-section)).

3. **Slots 5–7 — Depth + range:** Remaining Tier 1/2 (BOG, PhenoSync, Daedalus) — each must add a dimension Slot 1 didn't show (GradGPT: "promotion rule when tied — promote whichever adds a dimension").

4. **Slots 8–10 — Human texture / breadth without dilution:**
   - One service (teaching adaptation > crowdfunding logistics — pick the one with a specific human scene).
   - One community/leadership that shows *presence not title* (Sports Analysis Hub) OR supervised professional work (MalkansView) — not both.
   - **Leave blank rather than pad.** "An empty slot costs nothing. A 9th-grade club you attended twice dilutes entries above it." — GradGPT via Vault 11.

### 3.2 Common Mistakes to Avoid (GreatCollegeAdvice + Ivy100)

- **Unrealistic hour inflation:** 20+15+15+10 = 60 hrs/week on top of school = instant credibility loss. AOs do the math.
- **Chronological ordering:** Grade 9 volleyball first because it was first = wastes anchor.
- **Padding to 10:** "A student with 6 strong activities filled slots 7–10 with three one-time events and a brief summer job with no description. The cleaner version: 6 well-described activities, four blank slots. Admissions officers don't penalize students for having six instead of ten." — Ivy100
- **Cross-check vs essays:** Activity order must reinforce essay themes. If essays are about trustworthy systems + teaching adaptation, the list should front-load those proofs.

## 4. Common App 10 vs MIT 4 — What Changes

| Dimension | Common App (10) | MIT (4) |
|---|---|---|
| **Prompt** | "in order of importance to you" | "only enough space to list four things — please choose the four that mean the most to you" (MIT official) |
| **Strategy** | 10 = anchor (1) + reinforce (2–4) + depth (5–7) + human breadth (8–10) | 4 = four *different functions*, not four tech variations (master doc: GASI / ATLAS-or-Glassbox / Quant Finance Club-Future Lab / rural teaching-or-goalkeeping) |
| **What to cut** | Duplicates merged; Zeus/NTK/IPQ demoted to Additional Information | Anything that duplicates an essay's story — entry stays factual, essay carries reflection (Vault 11 §7.1/8.2) |
| **How to handle long research** | Keep 150c factual + "(see Add'l Info)" overflow (College Essay Mentor pattern); Additional Information now **300 words** (down from 650, 2025-26) | Same tightness, but AO reads it *with* 100-200-word essays — entry tees up essay, doesn't repeat it |
| **Hours** | Must be honest averages over weeks activity actually ran (Fall sport 12 hrs × 14 wks, not × 52) — Shemmassian | Four entries heavily scrutinized against transcript/teacher recs for consistency within territory |

> **"Instead of building a list and then deciding what to highlight, MIT applicants have to decide what story they're telling first, then choose the four activities that prove it. If an activity wouldn't survive as one of your top four, it almost certainly shouldn't be on your MIT application at all."** — Crimson (MIT Extracurriculars)

> **"MIT evaluates extracurriculars through territorial reading and cross-checking against the rest of the file. A stated passion for biology research is tested against what the science teacher writes, what the transcript shows, and what the essays say."** — Crimson

**For Aditya — MIT 4 recommendation (four functions):**
1. **GASI** — Tier 1 research, threshold + Silence Signature (proves intellectual initiative)
2. **Glassbox SRE *or* ATLAS** — production system with evaluation harness (Glassbox aligns with MIT Problem/Community Impact essay: trustworthy systems; ATLAS shows institutional-grade quant breadth — choose one, not both)
3. **Quant Finance Club / Future Lab** — leadership infrastructure (25–30 members, 13-week curriculum, 150 stocks, OU dynamics)
4. **Rural Education *or* Goalkeeping** — human/service vs athletic accountability (rural teaching pairs with Princeton service / VT Ut Prosim essays; goalkeeping gives clean "lonely seconds → recovery" for MIT Fun/Challenge)

Backup: Daedalus can replace Glassbox/ATLAS if engineering portfolio (Maker Portfolio) is submitted.

## 6. How to Use This + Vault 11 to Rewrite

1. **Order first, describe second.** Lock 10 + MIT 4 order per §3.1 before writing a single 150c line.
2. **Apply Vault 11 §2.1 formula per entry:** Verb (first word, no I) + Specific What + Scale + Outcome. One technical mechanism max per 150c.
3. **Variety sweep:** No verb repeats across 10 (Built vs Engineered vs Designed vs Founded vs Taught etc.); no corporate-verb inflation.
4. **Honest hours:** Use Hours/Weeks fields for time; don't burn 150c on hours that belong in fields. Averages over weeks actually active.
5. **Add'l Info pointer:** If methodology doesn't fit 150c, end with "(see Add'l Info)" and expand in 45-75 words, factual, no story arc (College Essay Mentor taxonomy — Vault 11 §7).
6. **Read slots 1–3 aloud:** Do they tell who you are without reading essays? If an entry could belong to a thousand STEM applicants, it fails master doc sentence-level test and must return to a specific object.

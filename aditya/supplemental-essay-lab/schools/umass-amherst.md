## 1. OFFICIAL 2026-27 PROMPTS

UMass Amherst uses the Common Application and asks for three short UMass-specific answers, all within a single required application essay process. The official UMass admissions page lists the three questions by topic (not as one continuous block of copy), so the exact on-screen Common App wording is confirmed from multiple third-party guides and noted separately below.

### 1.1 Official page wording (source: umass.edu/admissions/first-year-application-instructions)

The official First-Year Application Instructions page, under "Essay," states verbatim:

> "In addition to the essay, you are required to submit short answers to three UMass-specific questions.
>
> 1. Why do you want to attend UMass Amherst?
> 2. Why did you choose the major you did?
>
> The third question depends on whether or not you're interested in joining Commonwealth Honors College. If yes:
>
> * Why do you want to be part of Commonwealth Honors College?
>
> If no:
>
> * Tell us about a community you are a part of and explain how, as a product of this group or community, you would enrich our campus."

Source URL: https://www.umass.edu/admissions/first-year-application-instructions

Note: the official page does NOT publish the word limit. The 100-word limit for each answer is stated consistently by College Essay Guy, CollegeVine, Ivy Coach, College Essay Advisors, and Appybara (see Section 1.2 and 1.3). Treat the 100-word figure as UNVERIFIED against the official page but corroborated by five independent guides.

### 1.2 Full Common App prompt wording (as published by third-party guides)

**Prompt 1 (Why UMass):**

> "Please tell us why you want to attend UMass Amherst? (100 words)"
Source: College Essay Guy, https://www.collegeessayguy.com/blog/umass-amherst-supplemental-essays (page dated 08/10/2026); CollegeVine, https://blog.collegevine.com/how-to-write-the-umass-amherst-essays; Ivy Coach, https://www.ivycoach.com/the-ivy-coach-blog/college-essays/university-of-massachusetts-supplemental-essay-prompts/ ; Appybara, https://appybara.org/blog/university-of-massachusetts-amherst-supplemental-essays

**Prompt 2 (Why major):**

> "Please tell us why you chose the Major(s) you did? (100 words)"
Source: College Essay Guy (same URL above); Appybara (same URL above).
Note on punctuation variance: Ivy Coach renders this prompt as "Please tell us why you chose the Major(s) you did. (100 words)" with a period. CollegeVine renders it "Please tell us why you chose the Major(s) you did. (100 words)". The official UMass page renders the topic as "Why did you choose the major you did?" These are minor punctuation differences and the intent is identical. Mark the exact terminal punctuation UNVERIFIED.

**Prompt 3A (Community, for applicants NOT indicating Honors College interest):**

> "At UMass Amherst, no two students are alike. Our communities and groups often define us and shape our individual worlds. Community can refer to various aspects, including shared geography, religion, race/ethnicity, income, ideology, and more. Please choose one of your communities or groups and describe its significance. Explain how, as a product of this community or group, you would enrich our campus. (100 words)"
Source: College Essay Guy (same URL above); CollegeVine (same URL above).

**Prompt 3B (Honors College, for applicants indicating interest in Commonwealth Honors College):**

> "Commonwealth Honors College is a community in which students examine complex questions, learn across differences, and connect their academic interests to the world around them. What is one perspective, experience, or question you would bring to this community, and how would you hope to develop it at UMass Amherst? (100 words)"
Source: College Essay Guy (same URL above); Appybara (same URL above).

## 2. WHAT THIS SCHOOL'S READERS SAY THEY WANT

### 2.1 Official UMass admissions guidance (verbatim)

> "This is a required part of the application. Take your time writing your essay. Get feedback from your parents, a favorite teacher, or your school counselor. Proofread it carefully. ### 2.2 UMass admissions counselor, Juan Martinez-Muñoz (verbatim, Q&A)

> "Making an essay stand out can be a very nerve-racking thing to think about. Honestly, the best thing to do is to focus less on "standing out", and more on making sure you're presenting yourself as you truly are. Stories of growth and discovery definitely help essays stand out – as well making sure your formatting is proper helps a bunch too!"

> "It's tough to say what every college looks for as no two schools are exactly the same. That said, we look for your basics like formatting, seeing if the essay connects to the prompt and things like that. We want to see your story as much as we want to see if you're able to write a decent essay."

> "By all means, keep it casual. When students try to use language that they don't normally use, we can tell, and while admirable, it ruins the entire flow of the essay."
Source: https://www.umass.edu/admissions/articles/student-vs-counselor-writing-common-application-essay

### 2.3 UMass student writer Avani (verbatim, same official Q&A)

> "Your personality, experiences, aspirations, passions, and everything else about you that cannot be stated elsewhere in the application should shine in your essay. Grammar mechanics is important but more importantly there should be a concept and something that admissions representatives can draw from the essay to learn about you."

> "Three years ago, what I found to be the most important part of an essay was the hook! Make sure you draw in the reader and stand out. Admissions counselors are reading so many essays during this season so don't make it repetitive but rather stand out with the opener."
Source: https://www.umass.edu/admissions/articles/student-vs-counselor-writing-common-application-essay

### 2.4 UMass student voice, "How I Wrote my College Essay" (verbatim, official site)

> "The best way to indualize yourself from such large applicant pools is through your college essay, so regardless of the significance of the topic you chose to write about, make sure your own voice can be heard from it."

## 4. TECHNIQUE PATTERNS

Each bullet is backed by a quoted line and a URL.

- **Inject a UMass-specific element into nearly every sentence.** Ivy Coach: "your goal is to inject a UMass Amherst-specific element into nearly every sentence." (https://www.ivycoach.com/the-ivy-coach-blog/college-essays/university-of-massachusetts-supplemental-essay-prompts/)
- **Run the delete test on any sentence that could describe another school.** Ivy Coach: "If you write a sentence that contains a detail that could apply to any other school, such as "small class sizes," a "culture of interdisciplinary curiosity," or "world-class faculty and resources," delete it and try again!" (same URL)
- **Keep the Why-Us essay non-academic; save major detail for the Why-major answer.** CollegeVine: "Because one of the other supplemental prompts focuses on why you chose your major, you should avoid any overlap in this essay." (https://blog.collegevine.com/how-to-write-the-umass-amherst-essays)
- **Name concrete, non-generic UMass specifics.** CollegeVine: "Including general information like the strength of an academic department or a prime location can convey a lack of interest and detract from your application." (same URL)
- **Cut rankings and praise adjectives.** CollegeVine: "avoid listing school rankings or hackneyed adjectives praising the school, as this can come across as disingenuous." (same URL)
- **Content beats lyricism at 100 words.** College Essay Guy: "Focus more on content than "beautiful" language. With only 100 words, you don't have much space to be lyrical and poetic about why UMass Amherst is the perfect fit for you." (https://www.collegeessayguy.com/blog/umass-amherst-supplemental-essays)
- **Lead with a real hook, quickly.** UMass student Avani: "the most important part of an essay was the hook! Make sure you draw in the reader and stand out... stand out with the opener." (https://www.umass.edu/admissions/articles/student-vs-counselor-writing-common-application-essay)
- **Sound like yourself, not a junior academic.** College Essay Advisors: "trying to sound like an academic (admissions wants to hear your authentic voice!)". (https://www.collegeessayadvisors.com/supplemental-essay/university-of-massachusetts-amherst-supplemental-essay-guide/)
- **Natural language, not thesaurus language.** UMass admissions counselor Juan Martinez-Muñoz: "By all means, keep it casual. When students try to use language that they don't normally use, we can tell, and while admirable, it ruins the entire flow of the essay." (https://www.umass.edu/admissions/articles/student-vs-counselor-writing-common-application-essay)
- **Give the community answer a forward half, not just a past half.** Appybara: "Focusing entirely on the past: Spending 80 words describing the community leaves you no room to explain how you will enrich the university." (https://appybara.org/blog/university-of-massachusetts-amherst-supplemental-essays)

## 6. PITFALLS FOR THIS SCHOOL

- **Generic Why-Us filler, UMass edition.** Ivy Coach: "If you write a sentence that contains a detail that could apply to any other school, such as "small class sizes," a "culture of interdisciplinary curiosity," or "world-class faculty and resources," delete it and try again!" (https://www.ivycoach.com/the-ivy-coach-blog/college-essays/university-of-massachusetts-supplemental-essay-prompts/)
- **Praising the school instead of connecting to it.** CollegeVine: "The use of generic adjectives praising the school (like "stellar") should be avoided... Don't waste words giving trite compliments to the University!" (https://blog.collegevine.com/how-to-write-the-umass-amherst-essays)
- **A laundry list of clubs instead of depth.** CollegeVine: "The first response also reads a bit like a laundry list of extracurriculars. Rather than focusing on quantity, you should try to enhance the quality of your response by adding depth through detail." (same URL)
- **Course and professor names as shallow flexes.** Ivy Coach: "The names of courses or individual faculty members, though details that can be found in many essays submitted to this university, are not examples of strategic "Why UMass" specifics... each semester, the courses offered are subject to change, as are the professors teaching them." (https://www.ivycoach.com/the-ivy-coach-blog/college-essays/university-of-massachusetts-supplemental-essay-prompts/) Note: this is Ivy Coach's opinion, not UMass official guidance, and it conflicts with most "Why Us" advice. Handle by tying any named course to a specific reason, not by dropping the name.
- **Resume retread in the Why-major answer.** Appybara: "Summarizing your resume: Listing the advanced classes you took in this subject is redundant." (https://appybara.org/blog/university-of-massachusetts-amherst-supplemental-essays)
- **Vague ambition.** Appybara: "Vague ambitions: Saying you want to help people or make money lacks the depth required to stand out." (same URL)
- **Spending the community answer entirely in the past.** Appybara: "Focusing entirely on the past: Spending 80 words describing the community leaves you no room to explain how you will enrich the university." (same URL)
- **Repeating content across the three answers.** Appybara on the Honors prompt: "Repeating your main essays: Do not reuse the community or academic interest you just wrote about in the previous three prompts." (same URL)
- **Treating the famous dining rank as the hook.** UMass student profile names it as a reason students attend ("UMass Amherst is known for its number one dining"), but CollegeVine and Ivy Coach both warn that praise-of-the-school lines waste the word budget. Use dining only as texture, never as the substance.
  Source: https://www.umass.edu/admissions/articles/why-i-decided-attend-umass-amherst

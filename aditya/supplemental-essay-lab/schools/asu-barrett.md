## 4. TECHNIQUE PATTERNS

Each bullet is backed by a quoted line from a cited source.

- **Answer all three parts of the prompt, not just the first.** Barrett: "may take creative and intellectual risks, but be sure to address and integrate all elements of the prompt" (https://barretthonors.asu.edu/why-barrett/faqs). The "suggest you are a strong fit for Barrett" half is not optional.
- **Show how you reason, not what you achieved.** Barrett: "should give the admissions committee insight into how you reason and what you value" (https://barretthonors.asu.edu/why-barrett/faqs).
- **Be specific, with examples, not assertions.** CollegeVine relaying the Honors College: "offering specific examples in your claims" (https://blog.collegevine.com/how-to-write-the-arizona-state-university-essays). Source's own rule: "Anyone could say that—what you want to do instead is incorporate concrete examples" (same URL).
- **Generic is the named failure mode.** Barrett: "Essays that are general or impersonal are less successful." and first-year page: "Avoid impersonal or general essays." (https://barretthonors.asu.edu/why-barrett/faqs ; https://barretthonors.asu.edu/admissions/first-year-student).
- **Connect to Barrett specifically, not to college in general.** CollegeVine bad-example critique: "they fail to mention any specific resources, nor to explain why their past experiences draw them to Barrett, as opposed to any other school" (https://blog.collegevine.com/how-to-write-the-arizona-state-university-essays).
- **Pass the name-swap test.** CollegeVine: "If you have lines that could easily apply to another school if you just swapped the name out, that's a red flag that you need to get more specific." (same URL).
- **Open on a concrete moment, not a thesis statement.** CollegeVine on the strong example: "This specific, personal example shows how the student's experiences have shaped their values" and, on the weak one, it "reads more like a list of accomplishments than a reflection" (same URL).
- **Tie the essay to Barrett's stated values.** Barrett lists its guiding values as "Courage and Curiosity, Leadership and Agency, and Community and Belonging", and says the essay "will be most impactful if it is informed by" them (https://barretthonors.asu.edu/why-barrett/faqs). The About page lists them as "Courage & Curiosity / Belonging & Community / Agency & Leadership" (https://barretthonors.asu.edu/about).
- **Let reflection carry the ending instead of declaring the lesson.** CollegeVine on the strongest public example: it "comes full circle by relating the writer's research goals with the movie. Doing this gives the reader closure" (https://blog.collegevine.com/arizona-state-essay-examples). This matches the general AO rule that insight should be earned, not announced (`essay-lab/10-ao-expert-analysis.md`).
- **Use the whole 300-500 words, and get to the Barrett connection quickly.** CollegeVine: "500 words is on the long side for a supplement, but you shouldn't have a lengthy preamble—get right to connecting your interest to Barrett's offerings." (https://blog.collegevine.com/how-to-write-the-arizona-state-university-essays).

## 6. PITFALLS FOR THIS SCHOOL

- **The generic honors essay.** Barrett: "Essays that are general or impersonal are less successful." https://barretthonors.asu.edu/why-barrett/faqs
- **Listing accomplishments instead of reflecting.** CollegeVine on its weak example: "the student doesn't explore these dimensions, and as a result, the essay reads more like a list of accomplishments than a reflection on their core values." https://blog.collegevine.com/how-to-write-the-arizona-state-university-essays
- **Values as labels, not lived proof.** CollegeVine: "don't just say, 'I value leadership because I believe in empowering others.' Anyone could say that" (same URL).
- **Barrett boilerplate that could fit any school.** CollegeVine: "If you have lines that could easily apply to another school if you just swapped the name out, that's a red flag" (same URL).
- **Name-dropping resources without relevance.** CollegeVine: "Don't just randomly list programs and resources at Barrett. You need to explain directly why they're relevant" (same URL).
- **Picking a value pair or prompt because it "sounds good."** CollegeVine: "Admissions officers can tell when you're not being authentic." (same URL). This applies to the current three prompts too.
- **Missing a prompt element.** Barrett: "be sure to address and integrate all elements of the prompt" (https://barretthonors.asu.edu/why-barrett/faqs).
- **Treating Barrett like ASU (easy admission).** CollegeVine: "While ASU as a whole has a high acceptance rate, that rate does not reflect the difficulty of being admitted to Barrett." (https://blog.collegevine.com/how-to-write-the-arizona-state-university-essays).
- **AI-drafted prose.** Barrett: "AI should not be used." (https://barretthonors.asu.edu/why-barrett/faqs). See also the general AI red flags in `essay-lab/10-ao-expert-analysis.md` (flat rhythm, generic emotional beats, hollow vulnerability).

## 7. REVIEW OF MY OUTLINE

**Grade for the OUTLINE: A-**

The outline is genuinely well aimed and shows unusual self-awareness, but its Barrett-specific layer is thin and its central scenes lean on numbers that are not yet anchored to verified sources. It is an A- because a small amount of specific, verified detail would push it to A, and the identified risks are fixable without changing the plan.

### What the outline gets right

- **It picks the strongest prompt for this applicant.** The outline's recurring question and the official Prompt 1 line up exactly: the outline quotes "What is a question you find yourself returning to repeatedly?" and Barrett's Prompt 1 is "What is a question you find yourself returning to repeatedly? ..." (https://barretthonors.asu.edu/why-barrett/faqs). A builder who tests his own results is a natural owner of this prompt.
- **The question maps to Barrett's guiding values.** "What would prove me wrong?" is a courage-and-curiosity question, and the plan's emphasis on "other people entering the reasoning before the answer feels finished" touches Leadership and Agency and Community and Belonging. Barrett names exactly these values as the ones the essay should be "informed by" (https://barretthonors.asu.edu/why-barrett/faqs).
- **It keeps the ending unresolved.** The outline says "Keep the ending unresolved: a clean result will still tempt Aditya, so the question must be asked again rather than celebrated as a permanent virtue." This matches the general AO rule that insight should be earned, not announced (`essay-lab/10-ao-expert-analysis.md`), and it matches the strongest public Barrett example's "comes full circle" closure without a slogan (https://blog.collegevine.com/arizona-state-essay-examples).
- **It flags its own AI-shaped phrase.** The note "Avoid the generated review's phrase 'corrective I installed.' It sounds like software applied to a personality." is exactly the right instinct given Barrett's "AI should not be used" rule and the AI-tell vocabulary tracked in `essay-lab/10-ao-expert-analysis.md`.

### Weak planning choices, quoted, and what would strengthen them

- **Barrett fit is generic.** The outline writes "Barrett fit should center on other people entering the reasoning before the answer feels finished. Human Event can supply disagreement across readings; faculty criticism and an honors cohort can interrupt Aditya's habit of reviewing his own work too late." This is concept-level, not school-specific, and names no real mechanism. The CollegeVine critique of a weak Barrett essay is that "they fail to mention any specific resources, nor to explain why their past experiences draw them to Barrett, as opposed to any other school" (https://blog.collegevine.com/how-to-write-the-arizona-state-university-essays). Strengthen it by naming one verified Barrett mechanism: the Human Event's "critical thinking, discussion and writing" seminar (https://catalog.asu.edu/policies/barrett), the defended honors thesis with "a thesis director and second committee member" (same URL), or honors enrichment contracts (https://barretthonors.asu.edu/academics/first-year-entry-curriculum). Then say what a person in that structure would concretely do to him.
- **The scenes assert specificity without supplying it.** The outline says "thirty days felt too simple, so Aditya tested 20-day and 60-day alternatives and extra variables" and "The alternatives diluted the signal; the simpler choice survived." These are strong, but they are described in the abstract and do not match the verified research on file.

> "Be memorable. Use your essay to show who you are and what matters to you."

> "Be direct. You only have a few paragraphs, so make every word count."

> "Be focused. Do not repeat things already in your application. Explain what you’ll bring to Illinois."

> "Be yourself. Be honest and use real examples from school or activities."

> "Your essays are a place to share your story, passions, and ideas, as well as any context behind your academic path."

> "Fit With Your Major: We consider how your background, interests, and goals align with the major you selected."

> "Depth matters more than quantity, so focus on what’s meaningful to you."

If you’re not admissible to your first choice, you’ll be reviewed for your second choice."

### 3.1 Confirmed accepted, UIUC (Source 4: CollegeVine, "UIUC Essay Example: Analysis + Breakdown")

If you’re applying to the Division of General Studies, explain your academic interests and strengths or your future career goals. ## 4. TECHNIQUE PATTERNS

Each bullet is tied to a quoted line. Quotes come from Sources 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 13.

1. **One experience, not a survey.** "Explain, in detail, an experience you’ve had in the past 3 to 4 years related to your first-choice major." (Source 1) College Transitions reads this tightly: "“In detail” means one experience, not a survey of three." (Source 13)
2. **Open mid-experience with a detail only you could have lived.** GradGPT: "Open mid-experience with a concrete detail that only you could have lived." (Source 9)
3. **Show a failure or a fix, not just a win.** GradGPT's "Why it works": "One scrappy side project beat every class. Real engagement looks like this." (Source 9)
4. **Name a real Illinois resource and the unglamorous first step.** GradGPT: "Fit works when it is specific: a real lab, course, or program tied to your exact goal carries more weight than broad ambition." (Source 9)
5. **Do not repeat the activities list.** Official Illinois: "Do not repeat things already in your application. Explain what you’ll bring to Illinois." (Source 1)
6. **Depth over breadth.** Official Illinois: "Depth matters more than quantity, so focus on what’s meaningful to you." (Source 2)
7. **Give the second choice its own evidence.** College Transitions on UIUC: "if your first choice is computer science, where the admit rate runs near 7%, your second choice is doing most of the work in your application." (Source 13)
8. **Career goals must be concrete, and "societal impact" is the keyword Illinois CS reviewers appear to watch.** Reported rep: "a lot of people say they want to make apps that help people and that my career goals weren't solid enough"; a commenter: "my essay was about societal impact and i got in." (Source 12)
9. **One vivid, specific line can carry a whole short answer.** From the accepted urban-planning essay's opening: "Ever since I was a kid, I’ve had a problem with stepping on bugs on the sidewalk." (Source 4)
10. **Reflection must connect the experience to the field.** Research.com: "The essay should show a pattern between what you have already done and what you want to study next." (Source 15)

## 6. PITFALLS FOR THIS SCHOOL

1. **Generic "change the world through technology" with no path.** Research.com: "A student who explains how building a low-cost irrigation sensor changed their view of agricultural engineering may be more persuasive than a student who writes broadly about 'changing the world through technology.'" (Source 15)
2. **Vague coding-club and hackathon topics without your specific contribution.** Research.com: "A robotics captain who only says 'I led the team' is less compelling than a team member who explains how they redesigned a sensor mount after repeated failures and learned to test assumptions." (Source 15)
3. **Underspecified goals in the Illinois CS pool.** The reported rep rejected a qualified applicant because "a lot of people say they want to make apps that help people and that my career goals weren't solid enough." (Source 12)
4. **Repeating the activities list.** Official Illinois: "Do not repeat things already in your application." (Source 1)
5. **Praising Illinois generically.** College Transitions: "a response that would work for any large public university is a wasted opportunity in a way it would not be elsewhere." (Source 13)
6. **Treating the second choice as a formality.** College Transitions: "An answer that reads as an afterthought tells the committee you would rather not be there." (Source 13)
7. **Wasting words on a childhood origin story.** College Transitions: "“the past 3 to 4 years” rules out the childhood origin story, which is where a surprising number of applicants spend their opening sentences." (Source 13)
8. **Calling Math & CS / Stats & CS "CS + X."** Official: "The Mathematics & Computer Science (Math & CS) and Statistics & Computer Science (Stats & CS) degrees programs are not considered CS + X." (Source 26)
9. **Assuming a CS blended major is available as a second choice.** Illinois list of majors unavailable as a second choice, verbatim: "Computer Science (including all Computer Science blended majors)." (Source 3) This is a live risk for a CS-first applicant who wants a CS-adjacent second choice. See Section 7.
10. **Unexplained jargon.** The activity list's own rule ("no method names") applies here too: "OU mean-reversion," "Callaway–Sant'Anna DiD," and similar terms should not appear raw in a 150-word supplement. (Internal convention, `activities/activity-list.md`.)

## 7. AUDIT OF THE CURRENT DRAFT

File audited line by line: `supplements/us/uiuc/essays.md` (Draft 1). Graded against Sections 2-6. No rewrite below. No invented facts.

### Overall grade: B+ (strong raw material, two real risks: an unverified second-choice mechanism and a mislabeled major category)

### Response 1, "Experience related to your major" (NEXUS): A-

What works. It opens mid-scene with a claim only this applicant could make: "I built a stock market that does not exist." It stays inside one project and gives a real number ("orders cleared in about seven microseconds") and a real calibration claim ("Calibrated runs reproduce the nine patterns I test for, including fat-tailed returns and volatility clustering"). It closes on a field-level insight, matching Technique 10 and the accepted essay's spark-to-major motion in Section 3.1.

Weak lines and why they fail.
- "nine kinds of traders: herders, momentum traders, attention-limited traders, market makers, and more." The "and more" is filler that spends words without adding evidence, and it undersells a verified strength. The activity list has the full set ("nine trader types"), so a concrete second clause would fix it.
- "the nine patterns I test for." "Nine patterns" is undefined for a reader who has never seen the project. The activity list supplies the fix ("9/9 on the calibrated seed, 8.2/9 average across seeds"), which would make the claim auditable instead of mysterious.
- The reflection ("turning a messy human system into rules, then finding where those rules fail") is good but generic across CS. A single line tying it to the specific attention finding would raise this to A.

### Response 2, "Goals after graduating" (ATLAS, CS 442, PURE): B+

What works. It is concretely measured: "Three people now use ATLAS, my quant research platform, for their own work." It names a real Illinois course (CS 442, verified) and a real research route (PURE, verified), which satisfies Technique 4 and the official "Fit With Your Major" language in Section 2. The closing ("tools that show their reasoning, not just their outputs") is a clean, non-generic goal.

Weak lines and why they fail against Section 5.
- "CS 442: Trustworthy Machine Learning is the course I most want." Verified correct (Sources 16, 17). Keep. But the essay stops at the course title. Section 5 gives verifiable depth (the official description names "adversarial attacks," "differential privacy," and "data valuation"; the Spring 2026 instructor is Han Zhao). Citing one real mechanism, not the title alone, is what Technique 4 demands.
- "Through PURE, I want to join a faculty research team early, as a junior member learning how a lab tests its own results." Correct program, but it does not name a lab or research area, so it reads as a template. The Siebel research page lists ISUR, CS STARS, SRP, UR2PhD and NCSA SPIN as concrete entry points, and Section 5 gives named research areas; anchoring to one would fix the vagueness.
- "confident mistakes are expensive" is a strong line; it is the only place the essay converts the trust theme into stakes, so it is doing a lot of work in a short space.

### Response 3, "Second-choice major" (Statistics & Computer Science): A- on prose, but flagged for a factual and eligibility risk

What works. It gives the second choice its own evidence rather than treating it as a backup, which is exactly what Section 4, Technique 7 and Section 6, Pitfall 6 require. The specifics are college-level and real: "bootstrap methods that respect how markets move together, corrections across 23 hypotheses, and a holdout I froze before looking at new data." That is the strongest single evidence cluster in the draft.

Weak lines and why they fail.
- Header claim, not essay body: "Statistics & Computer Science or Mathematics & Computer Science ("CS + X")." This mislabels the majors. Official Illinois: "The Mathematics & Computer Science (Math & CS) and Statistics & Computer Science (Stats & CS) degrees programs are not considered CS + X." (Source 26) Fix the header and any related framing; the essay body does not repeat the error, but the file's own assumptions do.
- ELIGIBILITY RISK (highest priority): Illinois lists as unavailable for a second choice, verbatim, "Computer Science (including all Computer Science blended majors)." (Source 3) Illinois elsewhere groups "CS, CS + X, Mathematics & CS, Statistics & CS, or Computer Engineering" under "Blended CS Degrees." (Source 30) That strongly suggests Statistics & Computer Science and Mathematics & Computer Science may themselves be unavailable as a second choice for a CS-first applicant. This is UNVERIFIED and must be confirmed with Illinois admissions before the draft's second-choice plan is trusted. If confirmed unavailable, Response 3 targets a program the application may not let the applicant select as a second choice, which would invalidate the draft's core strategy at the header level ("CS admits ~7%, so the second choice matters").
- "Even if I end up there instead of CS" is a hedge that slightly weakens the commitment Illinois asks for in Source 3 ("you should be interested in pursuing a degree in that field").

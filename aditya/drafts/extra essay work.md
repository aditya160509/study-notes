# Extra Essay Work — 2026–27 Application Supplement System

> **Superseded on 25 August 2026.** This file contains an older university list and stale prompt assumptions. Use [`supplement-master-material.md`](./supplement-master-material.md) as the current source of truth. Do not draft from the school table below.

## Purpose

The Common App personal statement is one master essay. The additional work should be managed as a supplement bank: a small set of strong, reusable pieces adapted truthfully to each university.

This file is a working tracker, not a promise that every prompt or word limit is final. Re-check each school’s application portal before submitting.

## The writing system

### Core application pieces

1. **Common App personal statement** — use the current final draft in `aditya/drafts/final draft essay.md`.
2. **Why major / intellectual curiosity** — why computer science, research, building, and questions that remain worth pursuing.
3. **Why university** — specific courses, labs, communities, professors, or traditions connected to a real goal.
4. **Community and contribution** — what Aditya notices, builds, explains, or changes for other people.
5. **Challenge and growth** — a truthful account of failure, uncertainty, repair, and changed practice.
6. **Identity and upbringing** — use only details that are personally meaningful; do not add cultural decoration.
7. **Joy / curiosity outside class** — a concrete activity with texture, not a résumé summary.
8. **Short-answer bank** — favorite things, influences, current curiosity, fun, disagreement, and one-line self-description.

### Reusable story inventory

| Story | Best uses | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Pen-and-paper pseudocode exam | Challenge, growth, why coding changed | Keep the real exam details; do not invent drama |
| A-Level Central and first AI-built project | Why CS, curiosity, making | AI is a tool; do not claim it taught coding instantly |
| Research paper rebuild | Intellectual curiosity, failure, integrity | Keep the unresolved tension; do not turn it into a résumé flex |
| Teacher conversation | Accountability, growth, mentorship | Teacher was relaxed and skeptical, not angry |
| Thirty-day attention measure | Research method, perseverance, simplicity | Explain why the simple measure survived testing |
| JFS fit rejection and BIR review | Setback, communication, audience | Do not state that fit was the only reason for rejection |
| Gita and wanting publication | Identity, values, uncertainty | It did not erase desire; it gave the wanting somewhere to go |
| Reaching beyond understanding | Closing idea, future direction | Avoid making it a generic ambition statement |

## Recommended writing order

1. Finish and freeze the Common App personal statement.
2. Draft one strong **Why CS / intellectual curiosity** essay.
3. Draft one **community and contribution** essay.
4. Draft one **challenge and growth** essay without repeating the Common App arc mechanically.
5. Draft one **Why university** template using only replaceable evidence blocks.
6. Draft MIT, Caltech, Princeton, Yale, Stanford, and Penn first because they have the largest or most distinctive writing load.
7. Adapt the strongest pieces for Brown, UIUC, Dartmouth, Georgia Tech, Florida, USF, and Texas Tech.
8. Complete short answers last, using the short-answer bank and checking for repetition across the whole application.

## Prompt-to-piece matching

| Prompt type | Start from | What must change |
|---|---|---|
| Why this major? | A-Level Central + research question | Add the university-specific next question |
| Why this university? | Research and building goals | Name real programs and explain the fit |
| Community | Teaching, organizing, building, or sharing | Show another person’s experience, not only Aditya’s action |
| Challenge | Exam failure or research rebuild | Make the changed behavior concrete |
| Intellectual curiosity | Thirty-day measure and alternatives | Explain what kept the question alive |
| Identity / values | Gita and desire | Keep the admission of wanting; avoid a moral lesson |
| Fun / short answer | A real object, habit, question, or small obsession | Be specific and easy to remember |

## Working rules

- Never copy a supplement unchanged when the prompt asks for school-specific fit.
- Do not repeat the full Common App story in every supplement.
- Every “Why us?” claim needs a real source: course, lab, professor, student group, or program.
- Keep the student’s actual voice; remove consultant-like slogans and inflated conclusions.
- Do not invent dialogue, physical details, motivations, research results, or cultural details.
- Track word counts after every substantial edit.
- Keep a fact-check note beside any claim about a university or program.
- Re-check prompts, word limits, and deadlines in the live application portal before submission.

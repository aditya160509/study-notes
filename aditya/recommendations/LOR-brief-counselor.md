## 1. How I have grown

- **From ideas to finished work.** In Grade 10 I understood ideas quickly but often struggled to turn them into finished work. Over the last two years I have learned to carry a project all the way to the point where other people can use it, review it or build on it. - **From fast to careful.** I used to be satisfied the moment something worked. Now I check that I understand why it works before I call it done, and I go back to the parts I skipped on the first pass. - **From working alone to inviting criticism.** Early on I kept my work to myself until it felt finished, partly because I did not want anyone to see it half done. Now I share it early, with teachers, reviewers and teammates, because their questions find problems I would miss on my own. I have learned that being questioned early costs far less than being wrong late.

## 4. My research

- **Investor attention and volatility.** Across 21 markets and eleven years, I found that prices become much more unstable once trading passes about 1.39 times its normal level, while below that point extra attention makes little difference. The paper went through full peer review at *Borsa Istanbul Review*, a Q1 journal where most authors are university researchers. I used the reviewers' comments to restructure it and resubmitted it.
- **Skill and luck in Fantasy Premier League.** This paper treats the game's millions of managers like investors and asks whether they reward skill or luck when choosing players. I set my tests before running them and checked the main result on data the model had not seen. It is under review at the *Journal of Sports Economics*, also a Q1 journal.
- **PhenoSync.** A climate study showing that blueberries and their main bumblebee pollinator are drifting apart in time, built by combining NASA satellite, NOAA climate and citizen-science data. Much of the work was cleaning other people's data and being honest about regions where it was too thin. It was entered in the Oxford Saïd Business School Climate Challenge.
- **My role.** I am the corresponding author on all my papers, so I handle submission, reviewer comments and revision myself. That means writing to editors, responding to criticism point by point and deciding what to change. I also published an architecture paper on SSRN for my stock exchange simulator.

> *Example:* "It is rare for a school student to have work reviewed at one Q1 journal. Aditya has papers at two, both as corresponding author."

## 5. What I build

- **An engineering platform for my F1 team (Daedalus).** I built it alone, and my STEM Racing team uses it to design and compare cars before they are built, which saves time and material. It shows which assumption produced each result, so teammates can question a number instead of simply accepting it. The team has used it to choose between designs, including a case where a teammate's challenge changed our decision. For the national finals I upgraded it so that it recommends which simulation to run next, predicts race time from measured thrust and friction, checks those predictions against real track runs and checks each part against the rules using actual measurements.
- **A stock exchange simulator (NEXUS Exchange).** Nine kinds of automated traders buy and sell from one another on it, each following rules I had to write down exactly. I built it to test my research finding from a completely different direction. It taught me that turning a vague idea into code shows every gap in the explanation.
- **A research platform (ATLAS) and an outage tool (Glassbox).** ATLAS is a platform other people use for real analysis, so it has to keep working when I am not watching it. Glassbox uses AI to diagnose why software fails, and every number it gives points back to its evidence so that nothing is invented. Both have taught me to build for users other than myself.
- **A mock market for my club (Future Lab).** About 150 companies that members value and trade, so they learn by doing rather than only listening. It turned abstract ideas like valuation into something students could argue about. Building it was as much about teaching as it was about programming.
- **Internship.** Three months at MalkansView, a stock research firm, analysing company fundamentals on live market data. It showed me how fast real decisions have to be made and how much depends on the numbers being right. It is a large part of why I care about checking models before anyone relies on them.

> *Example:* "Aditya does not stop at a working prototype. He finishes things to the point where other people depend on them."

- **Hard subjects.** When a topic is difficult for me, such as Further Mechanics, I work through it problem by problem until it stops feeling unfamiliar. I do not expect to understand everything immediately. I have learned that steady effort works better for me than waiting for something to click.

> *Example:* "When something goes wrong, Aditya's first move is to fix it, not to defend it."

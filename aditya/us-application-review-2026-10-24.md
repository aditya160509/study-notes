### UW–Madison: exact format and waiver procedure

Common App personal essay + one Wisconsin essay answering why UW–Madison and why the selected major(s). The Wisconsin essay maximum is **650 words**, with **300–500 recommended**. Your current individual-school draft is **393 words** and discusses both Computer Sciences and Data Science.

To request the English waiver after applying: Student Center → Student eForms / Admissions → **ELP Waiver Request**. Wisconsin must receive and process your transcript before it can review the request. The published page asks applicants to allow **14 business days** for review. Ask your school to document English as the primary language of instruction for all four secondary years. Your SAT 630 is not a substitute for this waiver. If the waiver is denied, Wisconsin accepts DET **115+**, IELTS **6.5+**, or qualifying TOEFL results. A pending request does not prove the application is complete; follow the portal checklist and materials deadline.

## Fix these factual issues before submission

1. **NEXUS throughput:** the latest activity row says “10M+ orders/sec”; its detailed description says roughly **145K orders/sec per call** and 6.7-microsecond latency. These may measure different workloads. Use one clearly specified, reproducible benchmark or remove the speed claim from the application. Neither figure was independently benchmarked in this review.
2. **Internship hours:** 35 hours/week × 12 weeks is **420 hours**, while the detailed description says roughly **140 hours**. Establish actual hours and correct the conflicting figure. Do not invent a reconciliation.
3. **Concurrent commitments:** attention research and FPL research each list 20 hours/week for 35 weeks, and ATLAS lists another 20 for 20 weeks. Map the dates and avoid counting the same research/programming time twice. This is a consistency check, not a claim the work was impossible.
4. **Journal status:** the detailed attention-research account says rejected after review, rebuilt, and resubmitted. Do not label this a formal “revise-and-resubmit” editorial decision unless that is what the journal issued. “Peer-reviewed, revised after rejection, resubmitted; decision pending” is clearer. Submitted is not accepted or published; SSRN is a preprint repository rather than journal peer review.
5. **Journal names as organizations:** distinguish independent research submitted to Borsa Istanbul Review / Journal of Sports Economics from employment or an appointed role at the journals. “Independent research; submitted to …” avoids ambiguity.
6. **Daedalus:** retain the specific CFD/wind-condition anecdote only if it describes what you actually implemented and your team actually did. The detailed activity description emphasizes a decision platform; it does not itself substantiate every CFD claim in the essay. This is a verification gap, not evidence the anecdote is false.
7. **LOR ownership:** briefs contain suggested “top student” comparisons and full sample letters. Teachers must choose their own comparisons and supply firsthand examples. For MIT RA, check the recommended balance of a math/science teacher and humanities/social-science/language teacher: CS plus an IPQ/science supervisor may not supply that balance. MIT recommends it; the reviewed deadline page does not call it an absolute two-category requirement.
8. **Version drift:** old packet, `pending-work.md`, `university-list.md`, `us-university-screen.md`, the combined EA file and individual essays disagree about schools, rounds and prose versions. Use the latest five-school EA selection and individual essays when pasting. Old percentages, obsolete speed claims and removed schools should not be copied into applications.

## 11.02 The September 2026 full test: what actually happens

Source: video 1. These are the observations that matter for pacing.

### Read and Select

- You get about **5 seconds per word**. There is no time to reason it out.
- A real word has a **meaning you can recall**. If a word looks plausible but has no meaning for you, choose **Not real**.
- Do not use "it looks like English" as your test. Fake words often follow normal spelling patterns.
- First instinct is usually right. Changing answers costs the next word's time.

### Fill in the Blanks

- About **6–9 sentences**, roughly **20 seconds per sentence**.
- Read the whole sentence before typing. The missing letters are only the end of the word.
- Use **collocation** first, grammar second:
  - *widely **acclaimed** as the best*
  - ***delay** our meeting until tomorrow*
  - *hardly **distinguish** the outline from the wall*
  - ***recommend** + noun / gerund*
- You can let the timer run out; you do not have to press Submit. Use the remaining seconds to check spelling.

### Listen and Type

- About **9 items**; each sentence can be played **three times**.
- **First play:** type every word you catch, even with gaps.
- **Second and third plays:** fill gaps and fix spelling, articles, plurals, tense endings.
- Punctuation matters: capital letter, full stop, comma before a question tag, question mark for questions.
- **Partial credit** is available. A missing comma does not zero the item; a blank does.
- Accents may vary — American, British, Australian, Irish. Train with more than one accent.
- Contractions and full forms are generally both accepted (*there's* / *there is*). Be consistent.

### Read and Complete

- About **3–6 passages**. The missing words are inside a continuous text, not separate sentences.
- Read the **title, first sentence and last sentence** first. They are complete and give the topic and tense.
- Look for **repeated words** and synonym chains; the passage often reuses its own vocabulary.
- Partial credit; one unknown word does not ruin the passage.

### Interactive Reading

- **Two passages**, one with **7 minutes** and one with **8 minutes**, **6 questions** each.
- Time sink: the first question. Spend **at most 2 minutes** on it, then move.
- The five question moves:
  1. **Choose the correct word** for each gap.
  2. **Select the sentence** that links the two parts of the passage.
  3. **Highlight the exact text** that answers the question.
  4. **Select the main idea**.
  5. **Choose the best title**.
- For highlight questions, highlight **only the answer phrase**, not the whole sentence. Over-highlighting can lose the point.
- For main-idea and title questions, eliminate options that mention things **not in the passage** (airport, travel, school) before comparing the remaining two.
- The passage does not change between questions. Do not reread the whole text; go back only to check a detail.

### Interactive Listening

- **Two scenarios**. Each has three parts:
  1. **Listen and Complete** — four incomplete sentences based on a short scenario.
  2. **Listen and Respond** — choose the next line in a conversation.
  3. **Summary** — write a summary in **75 seconds**.
- You can replay the scenario in part 1. The visible timer covers **parts 1 and 2**, so do not burn it all on part 1.
- **Do not click Continue early.** The moment you continue, the 75-second summary starts. Use any remaining time to reread the scenario and plan.
- Summary formula — three sentences:
  1. **Who + topic:** "I spoke to my professor about whether to apply for a job or a doctorate."
  2. **Key detail:** the advice, options or disagreement.
  3. **Outcome:** what was decided or recommended.
- In Listen and Respond, pick the line that fits **the scenario and the previous line**, not the most impressive sentence. Wrong answers are often grammatically fine but off-topic.

### Writing section

- **Write About the Photo:** three times, **60 seconds** each. Start typing immediately; there is no prep time.
- **Interactive Writing:** **5 minutes** for part 1, then a follow-up question with **3 minutes**. You cannot edit part 1 during part 2.
- Structure in the video: **topic sentence → reason 1 + example → reason 2 + example → short conclusion**.
- The follow-up is usually "How does advertising influence this?" or "What could be done?" Answer it directly and link it to part 1.

### Speaking section

- **Speak About the Photo:** **20 seconds** prep, **90 seconds** speaking.
- **Read Then Speak:** **20 seconds** prep, **90 seconds**, usually **three sub-questions**. Answer all three.
- **Interactive Speaking:** **6–8 questions**, **35 seconds per answer**, split across **two topics**. Click **Record**; the timer does not wait.
- The video's live answers show the target: **25–35 seconds of relevant detail**, not a one-sentence answer.

### Samples

- **Writing Sample:** **5 minutes**, aim for **140–180 words**; the official guide states no minimum. It is sent to universities.
- **Speaking Sample:** **3 minutes**. It is sent to universities.
- Treat both as admissions material, not throwaway tasks.

## 11.03 The photo framework

Source: videos 3 and 10. This is the highest-value template in the series because photos appear **four times** (three writing, one speaking).

### Write About the Photo — 3 sentences

| Sentence | Job | Grammar |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | General summary: what is the photo of? | *This photo shows …* |
| 2 | Actions: who is doing what? | Present continuous: *is/are + -ing* |
| 3 | Location: where are the things? | Prepositional phrases: *in the foreground, behind, to the left of* |

Optional fourth sentence: a **grounded speculation** (*If I had to guess, …*) or a **descriptive detail** (clothing, weather, mood).

Rules from the video:

- **Do not label.** "There is a teacher, there are kids, there is a monitor" is a list, not a description.
- Use **full sentences**, not notes.
- Use **adjectives and adverbs**: *a crowded indoor market*, *she is leaning forward intently*.
- One idea per sentence. Do not write a long sentence with three clauses and lose control.
- 60 seconds is enough for **2–3 sentences**. The first sentence alone meets the minimum; the rest raises the score.

### Speak About the Photo — 5 steps

| Time | Step | What to say |
|---|---|---|
| 0–15 s | Summary | What is the photo about? |
| 15–40 s | Actions | Who is doing what? Present continuous. |
| 40–55 s | Location | Foreground, background, left, right, behind, in the distance. |
| 55–75 s | Speculation | A reasonable guess **based only on visible evidence**. |
| 75–90 s | Bonus details | Clothing, weather, mood, colours, atmosphere. |

The steps are not a rigid order. Start with the summary, then choose the richest two or three steps. If your mind goes blank, describe **clockwise**: top left → top right → bottom right → bottom left.

**Speculation rule:** stay close to the photo. "She is preparing for an exam because she is surrounded by books" is safe. "Her parents forced her to study medicine instead of music" is inventing a story and can hurt task relevance.

### 30 photo phrases

| Phrase | Use |
|---|---|
| in the background | behind the main subject |
| in the foreground | closest to the camera |
| close-up | shot with lots of detail, from very near |
| wide shot | broad view showing the surroundings |
| vibrant | bold, lively colours |
| horizon | line where earth/sea meets sky |
| above the horizon | floating/flying higher than that line |
| dawn | first light in the morning |
| twilight | soft light after sunset |
| scorching | extremely hot |
| clear blue sky | cloudless blue sky |
| in the distance | far away |
| tracks | marks left by a person, animal or vehicle |
| overcast | completely covered by clouds |
| winding road | road that curves and twists |
| a winter's day / a summer's day | season + weather |
| monument | building/statue of historical importance |
| lit up | full of light |
| hustle and bustle | noisy, busy energy |
| features | verb: "the shot features …" |
| engaged in conversation | talking with focus |
| deep in discussion | absorbed in a conversation |
| in their 60s | approximate age |
| surrounded by | enclosed by things |
| in full bloom | flowers fully open |
| crowded with | full of people/things |
| tree-lined street | street bordered by trees |
| engrossed in work | fully concentrating |
| captures | "the image captures a moment" |
| bathed in sunlight | covered in bright natural light |

## 11.04 Writing: the paragraph formula

Source: videos 8, 11 and 12.

### Step 1 — identify the question type

| Type | Signal words | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Argument** | *Do you agree or disagree? Which do you recommend? Why?* | Take a side and defend it |
| **Describe** | *Discuss the implications. What are the effects?* | Explain two effects or features |
| **Recount** | *Describe a time … What have you learned …* | Tell a specific experience |

Task relevance is scored. A recount when the prompt asks for an argument loses points even if the English is good.

### Step 2 — one paragraph, three parts

| Part | Content |
|---|---|
| **Topic sentence** | Your opinion or focus + a controlling idea ("for two main reasons") |
| **Supporting sentences** | One or two reasons, each with an explanation, example, result or personal experience |
| **Concluding sentence** | Paraphrase the topic sentence; do not add a new idea |

**Topic sentence frames**

- *I completely agree with the statement that ______ for two main reasons.*
- *I strongly disagree with the idea that ______, as I believe ______.*
- *In my view, ______ is the better option because ______.*
- *The effects of ______ can be seen in two main areas: ______ and ______.*
- *One experience that shaped my view of ______ was ______.*

**Supporting frames**

- *Firstly, ______. This is because ______.*
- *For instance, ______.*
- *A clear example of this is ______.*
- *Personally, when I ______, I found that ______.*
- *Another reason is that ______. As a result, ______.*

**Conclusion frames**

- *In conclusion, ______.*
- *For these reasons, I believe ______.*
- *In summary, it is clear that ______.*

### Step 3 — time budget

**Interactive Writing, part 1 (5 minutes)**

| Time | Task |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:30 | Read the prompt; choose your side; pick two reasons |
| 0:30–4:00 | Write the paragraph |
| 4:00–5:00 | Proofread: articles, plurals, verb endings, punctuation |

**Interactive Writing, part 2 (3 minutes)**

| Time | Task |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | Read the follow-up; decide your direct answer |
| 0:15–2:30 | Write 3–5 sentences; link to part 1; add one new detail |
| 2:30–3:00 | Proofread |

**Writing Sample (5 minutes)** — same architecture, but it goes to universities. Use the full time, write 140–180 words if you can do it without losing accuracy, and leave 45 seconds to proofread.

### Vocabulary upgrades

| Basic | Better |
|---|---|
| important | crucial, significant, vital |
| problem | challenge, issue, obstacle |
| I think | in my opinion, it could be argued that |
| good | beneficial, effective, valuable |
| bad | harmful, detrimental, ineffective |
| a lot of | numerous, a considerable amount of |
| get | obtain, acquire, gain |
| show | demonstrate, illustrate, indicate |
| because | since, as, due to the fact that |
| but | however, nevertheless, whereas |

Do not force an advanced word you cannot spell or use naturally. Clear and correct beats impressive and wrong.

## 11.05 Speaking: PPF and OREO

Source: videos 7 and 9.

### PPF — for 90-second answers

Use **Present → Past → Future** on **one idea**. This gives you length, detail and a range of tenses without needing three new ideas.

**Question:** *Describe something you do to stay healthy.*

| Part | Content |
|---|---|
| **Present** | What you do now, how often, why |
| **Past** | When you started, what it was like then, a specific memory |
| **Future** | What you plan to do, why, how it will help |

**Frames**

- *One thing I do to ______ is ______. It involves ______. These days I ______.*
- *In the past, when I first started, I ______. I remember ______.*
- *In the future, I plan to ______ because ______.*

This is the default structure for **Read Then Speak** and any 90-second answer that asks about you. For the 3-minute **Speaking Sample**, use PPF once, then add a second idea or a counterargument so the answer does not become repetitive.

### OREO — for Interactive Speaking

Use **Opinion → Reason → Example → Outcome** for each 35-second answer.

| Part | Job | Frame |
|---|---|---|
| **O**pinion | Answer directly | *Yes, I think … / Personally, I believe …* |
| **R**eason | One main reason | *One main reason is that …* |
| **E**xample | A specific example | *For example, when I was … / In my country, …* |
| **O**utcome | Wrap up | *As a result, … / That is why I …* |

**Buy-time phrases** (use before the opinion if you need a second):

- *That's an interesting question.*
- *I've never really thought about that, but …*
- *Off the top of my head, I'd say …*
- *It depends on the situation, but generally …*

**Delivery rules**

- Interactive Speaking: **25–35 seconds**, not 5 and not 34 seconds of filler.
- Speak at a steady pace. A short pause is better than "um".
- Do not repeat the same idea in the next question. The test asks several questions on the same topic; add a new angle each time.
- You cannot replay the question. If you miss it, answer the topic you did hear and stay relevant.

### Transitions for three-part Read Then Speak

- *As for the second part of the question, …*
- *Moving on to ______, …*
- *To answer the final part, …*
- *On the question of ______, …*

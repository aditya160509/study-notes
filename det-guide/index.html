How the test works

The DET is one adaptive section, not four separate skill blocks. All question types are mixed together, and each answer changes the difficulty of the next one. Harder questions appearing is a good sign.

PartTimeWhat happens
1. Setup~5 minID check, webcam, phone as second camera (DET mobile app), room scan and ear scan to prove no earbuds.
2. Adaptive test~45 minAll graded question types in mixed order. This sets your overall score and subscores.
3. Writing & Speaking Samples~10 minOne essay and one video answer. Sent to every university you share scores with, and admissions officers do read and watch them.
July 2025 changes: Read Aloud and Listen, Then Speak were removed. Interactive Speaking was added. Interactive Listening now opens with Listen and Complete. Minimum answer times were removed from five tasks. Prep material from before mid-2025 describes an older test.
Late 2025 changes: the DET mobile app is now required as a secondary camera, and the test includes a room scan and an ear scan. A purchased test credit is valid for 21 days; you can buy up to three tests in a rolling 30-day period.

Scores and targets

You get one overall score plus eight subscores: four integrated ones (Literacy, Comprehension, Conversation, Production) and four single-skill ones (Reading, Writing, Listening, Speaking).

SubscoreCombinesFed mostly by
LiteracyReading + WritingRead and Select, Read and Complete, Fill in the Blanks, Listen and Type, Write About the Photo
ComprehensionReading + ListeningRead and Complete, Interactive Reading, Listen and Type, Interactive Listening
ConversationListening + SpeakingInteractive Listening, Interactive Speaking, Speak About the Photo
ProductionWriting + SpeakingWrite About the Photo, Interactive Writing, all speaking tasks

How writing and speaking are graded

CriterionWhat earns creditWhat loses credit
ContentAnswers the exact question; relevant developmentOff-topic, memorised, or only partly answers
Discourse coherenceClear order, paragraphs, effective linkersJumping between ideas; no conclusion
GrammarAccurate simple sentences plus complex structuresRepeated tense, article, agreement errors
LexisPrecise, varied vocabulary and collocationsBasic words repeated; wrong word forms
Fluency (speaking)Steady pace; natural pauses; few restartsLong silences; repeated "um"; abandoned sentences
Pronunciation (speaking)Clear and intelligible at first listenMumbling; unclear endings; flat intonation

Pronunciation is judged on clarity, not on sounding American or British. A clear accent is not penalised.

The 13 question types

Times and counts vary slightly by test because the test adapts.

Read and Select

Reading5 s per word · ~15–18×

One word appears. Click Yes if it's a real English word, No if it's made up.

  • Fake words look plausible but have odd letter patterns (flurment, cradive). Go with your first instinct.
  • Wrong "Yes" answers cost you. If you've truly never seen it and it looks off, choose No.
  • Train it: read academic word lists (AWL) and news articles; spelling patterns stick.

Fill in the Blanks

Reading~20 s · ~6–9×

A sentence with one unfinished word — the second half is missing. Type the ending so the whole word is correct.

  • Read the whole sentence first and work out the part of speech before guessing.
  • Look for collocations: make a decision, take responsibility, pay attention.

Read and Complete

Reading3 min each · ~3–6×

A paragraph where many words are cut in half. Type the missing letters.

  • Fill the easy gaps first, then use context for the hard ones.
  • Never leave a gap blank. Each word is scored separately, so a guess can earn partial credit.
  • Check grammar: plurals (-s), tense (-ed), and agreement. Spelling must match the word exactly; US and UK spellings are both accepted.

Interactive Reading

Reading2 sets · 7 min + 8 min

One passage, six sub-questions in sequence: Complete the Sentences, Complete the Passage, Highlight the Answer, Identify the Idea, Title the Passage.

  • For Highlight the Answer, select the exact phrase that answers the question, nothing extra.
  • For Title the Passage, pick the title that covers the whole text, not one paragraph.
  • The clock covers the whole set, so don't burn time on one sub-question.

Listen and Type

Listening1 min · up to 3 plays · ~6–9×

Hear a sentence, type it exactly.

  • Play 1: get the meaning. Play 2: fill gaps. Play 3: check small words.
  • Most lost points are missing articles, prepositions and plural -s.
  • Train it: dictation from podcasts or TED talks, 10 sentences a day.

Interactive Listening

Listening~6.5 min + 75 s summary · 2 sets

You play a student talking with a classmate or professor. Each set has: Listen and Complete (fill 3–4 blanks about the scenario), Listen and Respond (pick the best next reply, 5–6 turns), then Summarize the Conversation in writing.

  • Pick the reply that fits the tone and moves the conversation forward. Too casual with a professor is wrong.
  • Scenario audio can be replayed, but the timer keeps running.
  • For the summary, write 3–4 full sentences: who, the problem, what was suggested, what was decided.
The student spoke with [professor] about [topic/problem]. The professor suggested [advice 1] and [advice 2]. They also discussed [detail]. In the end, the student decided to [outcome].

Write About the Photo

Writing1 min · 3× in a row

Describe a picture in writing.

  • Aim for 2–3 complex sentences (~40–50 words). Leave 10 seconds to proofread.
  • Use precise nouns and verbs, prepositions of place, and one hedged inference.
In the foreground, [subject] is [action] while [second detail]. Behind them, [background details] can be seen. It appears to be [time/place], and the atmosphere seems [mood].

Interactive Writing

Writing5 min + 3 min · 1×

Answer a prompt, then answer a follow-up question about the same topic.

  • Part 1: ~120 words. Clear opinion, two reasons, one specific example each.
  • Part 2: ~80 words of new ideas. Restating Part 1 earns nothing.
  • Use a range of linkers (however, as a result, for instance, on the other hand) and a few less common words.
I believe that [position] for two main reasons. First, [reason], because [explanation]. For example, [specific example]. Second, [reason]. This matters since [consequence]. Although some people argue that [counterpoint], I think [rebuttal]. Overall, [restate position].

Speak About the Photo

Speakingup to 90 s · 1×

Describe a photo out loud.

  • Speak for about 60–90 seconds. Use the same order as the written version: foreground, background, inference.
  • Steady pace beats fast. A short pause is better than "um".

Read, Then Speak

Speaking20 s prep · up to 90 s · 1×

Read a prompt with bullet points, then answer it out loud.

  • Cover every bullet in the prompt, in order.
  • Use prep time to pick one real story. Specific details sound fluent.
I'd like to talk about [topic]. [Bullet 1: what/when]. [Bullet 2: what happened, with one detail]. The reason this stands out to me is [feeling/why]. Looking back, it taught me [lesson].

Interactive Speaking

Speaking35 s per answer · 1 set of 6–8

A video-chat style conversation with an animated character. Each question builds on the last. The question plays once, no prep time.

  • Listen fully before recording. You can't replay or go back.
  • Answer directly in the first sentence, then add one reason and one example.
  • Refer back to what you said earlier when it fits. It shows real conversation.
[Direct answer]. The main reason is [reason]. For example, [quick example]. So overall, [wrap-up].

Writing Sample

Sample30 s prep + 5 min · seen by schools

A short essay on a given topic.

  • Aim for 150+ words with a clear intro, two body points and a conclusion.
  • Write it like a mini application essay. Admissions readers see it raw.

Speaking Sample

Sample30 s prep · 1–3 min · seen by schools

Answer a prompt on video.

  • Look at the camera, sit in good light, use a plain background.
  • About 90 seconds of clear, structured speech beats 3 minutes of filler.

Rules that raise your score

RuleWhy
Never leave a blank emptyA guess can score; an empty gap can't.
Use the full time on writing and speakingLonger, more varied answers give the scorer more evidence of range.
Vary vocabulary and sentence typesGraders reward range: relative clauses, conditionals, passive voice, linkers.
Proofread every written answerSpelling and grammar errors feed Literacy and Writing directly.
Fluency over perfection when speakingSelf-correcting constantly hurts more than a small error.
Don't memorize whole answersOff-topic or memorized speech is flagged. Memorize structures only.
Ignore difficulty swingsHard questions mean you're doing well. Stay calm and keep going.

Worked examples

Use these to see the difference between a plausible answer and the answer the scorer wants. Cover the answer blocks until you have chosen.

Interactive Reading — “The Last Train”

Passage. Maya had planned to leave the laboratory by six, but the experiment refused to cooperate. At half past seven she was still adjusting the microscope, and the last train to Harrow left at eight fifteen. She packed her notebook into her bag, pulled on her coat, and hurried through the rain to the station. The platform was almost empty. A cleaner was mopping the far end, and a single vending machine hummed against the wall. Maya checked the departures board twice. The train was delayed by twelve minutes, which gave her time to buy a coffee and drink half of it before the announcement. When the train finally arrived, she chose a seat by the window and opened her notebook. The pages were full of numbers she had copied that afternoon, but one result still puzzled her. She had expected the reaction to slow down as the temperature rose; instead, it had become faster. She wrote a question in the margin and circled it. By the time the train reached Harrow, the rain had stopped. Maya walked home under a clear sky, thinking not about the delay but about the experiment.

1. Complete the Sentences

Reading

Choose the best word for each gap: “the experiment refused to (1) ___”; “she (2) ___ through the rain”; “the platform was almost (3) ___”; “drink (4) ___ of it”; “one result still (5) ___ her.”

Options: 1 cooperate / collaborate / coordinate / connect · 2 hurried / wandered / strolled / drifted · 3 empty / quiet / silent / vacant · 4 half / part / most / some · 5 puzzled / confused / disturbed / interrupted

Show answers

1 cooperate · 2 hurried · 3 empty · 4 half · 5 puzzled. “Refused to cooperate” is a collocation; “hurried” matches the rush; “almost empty” describes the platform; “half of it” is the natural quantity; “puzzled her” means confused her.

2. Complete the Passage

Reading

Which sentence best joins “She wrote a question in the margin and circled it” to “By the time the train reached Harrow, the rain had stopped”?

A. The train was now moving quickly through the countryside. · B. She closed the notebook and put it away for the rest of the journey. · C. Outside, the rain was still falling heavily against the window. · D. She had forgotten her umbrella at the laboratory.

Show answer

B. It links the note-taking to the later walk home. A does not bridge the two parts; C contradicts “the rain had stopped”; D is irrelevant.

3–4. Highlight the Answer

Reading

Q3: Why was Maya still in the laboratory at half past seven? Q4: What surprised Maya about the result?

Show answers

Q3: “the experiment refused to cooperate”. Q4: “She had expected the reaction to slow down as the temperature rose; instead, it had become faster.” Highlight the contrast, not just “it had become faster”.

5–6. Identify the Idea and Title

Reading

Main idea options: A. Maya missed the last train. B. A journey home is interrupted by rain. C. A researcher spends a journey thinking about an unexpected result. D. Trains are often delayed in bad weather.
Title options: A. A Delayed Train B. The Last Train C. Rain Over Harrow D. Maya's Laboratory

Show answers

Main idea: C. The passage is about the journey and the unresolved result. A is factually wrong; B and D focus on details. Title: B. It captures the setting and the journey; A is too narrow, C focuses on weather, D on one location.

Interactive Listening — office hours

Transcript. Student: Professor Lane, do you have a minute? I'm worried about the research proposal. Professor: Of course. Come in. What's the issue? Student: I've been ill for the past week, and I haven't been able to finish the literature review. I was hoping to ask for an extension. Professor: I see. The proposal is due on Friday. How much extra time do you need? Student: Would it be possible to have until the following Wednesday? Professor: That should be fine, but you'll need to email me a short outline by Friday so I can see your progress. Student: Of course. I'll send that by Friday morning. Thank you.

Listen and Complete

Listening

1. The student is worried about the ______. 2. The student has been ______ for the past week. 3. The professor asks for a short ______ by Friday.

Show answers

1 research proposal · 2 ill · 3 outline. Spelling is secondary here; the idea must be right.

Listen and Respond

Listening

Professor: “The proposal is due on Friday. How much extra time do you need?”
A. “As much as possible, please.” B. “Would it be possible to have until the following Wednesday?” C. “I don't know. What do you think?” D. “Friday is fine.”

Show answer

B. Specific, polite, and negotiable. A is vague; C pushes the decision back; D contradicts the request.

Summarize the Conversation

Writing + Listening

75 seconds, 3–4 complete sentences: who + problem → advice/decision → outcome.

Show model

The student met with Professor Lane because she had been ill and had fallen behind on the literature review for her research proposal. She asked for an extension beyond Friday, and the professor agreed to let her submit the proposal the following Wednesday. However, the professor asked her to email a short outline by Friday morning so that he could check her progress. The student agreed to send the outline and keep the professor updated.

Write About the Photo · 60 s

1. This photo shows ______ (who) ______ing in/at ______.
2. ______ is/are ______ing ______, while ______ is/are ______ing ______.
3. In the foreground/background/on the left, there is/are ______.
4. Optional: ______ looks ______ because ______.

2–3 full sentences · present tenses · adjectives/adverbs · location prepositions · no lists.

Speak About the Photo · 90 s

0–15   Summary: This photo shows…
15–40  Actions: … is/are …ing …
40–55  Location: In the foreground/background…
55–75  Speculation: If I had to guess, … because…
75–90  Detail: What stands out is…

If you freeze, go clockwise: top left → top right → bottom right → bottom left.

Interactive Writing · 5 min + 3 min

[Topic] I agree/disagree that ______ for two main reasons.
[Reason 1] Firstly, ______. This is because ______.
[Example] For instance, ______.
[Reason 2] Another reason is that ______. As a result, ______.
[Conclusion] In conclusion, ______.

Write part 1 until 4:00, then proofread. Part 2: direct answer → link to part 1 → new detail → close.

Read Then Speak · 20 s + 90 s

Present: One ______ I'd like to talk about is ______.
Past:    I first ______ when ______. I remember ______.
Future:  In the future, I plan to ______ because ______.
Close:   That is why ______.

PPF = Present → Past → Future. Answer every sub-question; use transitions.

Interactive Speaking · 35 s × 6–8

O  Opinion: I think ______.
R  Reason: One main reason is that ______.
E  Example: For example, ______.
O  Outcome: As a result, ______.

25–35 seconds. Click Record. New angle each question. No repetition.

Interactive Listening summary · 75 s

I spoke to ______ about ______.
He/She explained that ______ and suggested that ______.
In the end, we agreed that ______.

Capture: who · topic · key detail · outcome.

Study plans

About 60–90 minutes a day. Take the free official practice test on day 1 and again at the end of each week.

Week 1 · Baseline

  • Free practice test; note your weakest subscore
  • Learn every question type on this page
  • Daily: 50 Read and Select words, 10 dictation sentences

Week 2 · Reading & listening

  • Daily Read and Complete drills (3 passages)
  • Interactive Listening practice; write summaries in 75 s
  • Learn 20 academic words a day with collocations

Week 3 · Writing & speaking

  • 3 photo descriptions a day, timed at 60 s
  • 1 Interactive Writing + 1 Writing Sample a day
  • Record speaking answers; replay and cut filler words

Week 4 · Full mocks

  • 3–4 full timed mocks in real conditions
  • Fix your two weakest question types only
  • Do a full tech run: second phone, room scan, lighting

Test-day setup and checklist

The DET now uses a computer + smartphone setup. The phone is not optional: it connects through the DET mobile app, performs the room scan and ear scan, and stays in place as the secondary camera. Use the DET desktop app on the computer; do not assume a browser-only setup.

Secondary camera position

  • Phone to your side, slightly behind your shoulder, on a stable stand — not in your hand.
  • Your screen, keyboard, hands, and face must all be visible.
  • Lens at roughly desk height or slightly above; secure the cable so it cannot pull the phone.
  • Do not move or touch the phone once the test begins.

Room scan and ear scan

  • Show the desk, under the desk, walls, floor, and door — slowly, without fast panning.
  • Tie back hair; remove glasses, clip-on or magnetic earrings, and anything that could look like earbuds.
  • Touch the phone briefly to each ear when prompted. Headphones, earbuds, and headsets are prohibited.
  • Use one keyboard, one mouse, and one phone. Disable predictive text and proofreading; no VPN or virtual machine.
  • An incomplete scan is a common reason results are delayed or uncertified.

Ticks are saved in this browser only.

## 09.01 Source hierarchy

Not all DET advice is equally reliable. Use sources in this order:

| Priority | Source | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Official DET Question Guide** | Defines task counts, timings, scoring, adaptive status |
| 2 | **Official DET Sample Questions** | Shows the actual interface and task format |
| 3 | **Official DET website / help centre** | Rules, equipment, ID, results, institution search |
| 4 | **Official DET blog** | Format changes and setup requirements |
| 5 | **This guide** | Synthesis and practice strategy |
| 6 | Third-party prep sites / YouTube | Useful drills, but often outdated or speculative |

**Rule:** if a third-party source contradicts the official Question Guide, trust the official guide. Teacher Luke is useful for strategy and live demonstrations, not for official rules, prices or policy.

## 09.03 Free practice plan using only official materials

You do not need to pay for a course. This loop uses free official resources plus this guide.

### Day 1 — Baseline

1. Take the free practice test.
2. Record the overall range and all eight subscores.
3. Read the Question Guide sections for your two weakest tasks.

### Days 2–6 — Task cycle

| Day | Official resource | This guide | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | Practice test reading section | 02-reading.md | Error log |
| 3 | Sample questions: listening | 03-listening.md | 10 dictations |
| 4 | Sample questions: writing | 04-writing.md | 2 timed essays |
| 5 | Sample questions: speaking | 05-speaking.md | 6 recordings |
| 6 | Practice test full | 07-study-plan.md | Mock review sheet |

### Day 7 — Review

- Re-read your error log.
- Redo the two weakest task types.
- Take the practice test again.
- Compare subscores, not just the overall.

**Repeat weekly.** The official practice test is the best free benchmark because it uses the same task types and scoring model.

## 09.04 Third-party tools — how to judge them

Before paying for any prep product, check:

1. **Does it cover the post-July-2025 format?** It must include Interactive Speaking and Listen and Complete.
2. **Does it show the official task counts and timings?** If it says "Read Aloud" or "Listen, Then Speak", it is outdated.
3. **Does it grade writing/speaking against the four criteria?** A generic grammar score is not enough.
4. **Does it explain *why* an answer is wrong?** Explanations matter more than more questions.
5. **Does it promise a score?** No legitimate tool can guarantee a DET score.

**Use third-party tools for:** extra drill volume, timed practice, and feedback on writing/speaking.
**Do not use them for:** official rules, task counts, or score guarantees.

## 09.05 Reading sources by level

| Level | Sources | Why |
|---|---|---|
| B1–B2 | BBC Learning English, News in Levels, graded readers | Build fluency without overwhelm |
| B2–C1 | BBC Future, The Conversation, Scientific American, NYT Learning Network | Academic vocabulary and argument structure |
| C1–C2 | The Economist, Nature news, JSTOR Daily, university blogs | Dense syntax and precise lexis |

**How to read for DET:**

1. Read once for the main idea (60–90 seconds).
2. Write a one-sentence summary without looking back.
3. Underline five useful collocations.
4. Write one sentence using two of them.
5. Identify the author's attitude (positive, critical, neutral).

## 09.06 Listening sources by level

| Level | Source | Task |
|---|---|---|
| B1–B2 | 6 Minute English, TED-Ed | Dictation, 3 sentences |
| B2–C1 | BBC Global News Podcast, NPR Short Wave | Summary, 5 sentences |
| C1–C2 | University lectures (MIT OpenCourseWare, Oxford podcasts) | Note-taking + 75-second summary |

**Active listening routine (15 min):**

1. Listen once without pausing; write the main idea.
2. Listen again; note three details.
3. Listen a third time; transcribe two sentences exactly.
4. Compare with the transcript; mark missed small words.
5. Retell the clip in 60 seconds without notes.

## 09.07 Speaking and writing feedback without a tutor

### Speaking

- Record on your phone.
- Use the review sheet in [05-speaking.md](05-speaking.md).
- Transcribe 30 seconds of your answer; count filler words and repeated words.
- Re-record once, fixing one issue only.

### Writing

- Use the checklist in [04-writing.md](04-writing.md).
- Run a spell-check, but do not accept every grammar suggestion blindly.
- Read the essay aloud; awkward sentences become obvious.
- Compare your answer to the model answers, but do not copy them.
- Ask a fluent friend to mark **one** criterion (e.g. coherence), not everything.

## 09.08 What to ignore

- **"DET is just IELTS but shorter."** It is not; the tasks and scoring differ.
- **"You can cheat the adaptive algorithm."** You cannot; answer honestly and move on.
- **"Memorise these 10 essays."** Memorised answers are flagged and do not fit the prompt.
- **"Use headphones to hear better."** Prohibited; you must use speakers.
- **"Take the test repeatedly until you get lucky."** Limited to 3 per 30 days, and schools see each score you send.
- **"A 160 is required."** Most schools require far less; check the actual minimum.
- **"The Samples don't matter."** They are scored and sent to schools.

## 09.09 A minimal resource stack

If you are overwhelmed, use only these:

1. Official Question Guide (read once, refer back).
2. Official practice test (weekly).
3. This guide's 01, 04, 05, and 08.
4. One news source and one podcast.
5. A phone recorder and a notebook.

That is enough for a serious score improvement. More resources do not equal more progress; **deliberate repetition** does.

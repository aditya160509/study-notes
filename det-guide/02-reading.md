# 02 — Reading

Reading tasks feed **Literacy, Comprehension and Reading**. They are the most frequent questions, so small gains here move your score a lot.

---

## 1. Read and Select

**What happens:** One word appears for ~5 seconds. Click **Yes** (real English word) or **No** (fake).

**Strategy**
- Answer on first instinct. 5 seconds is too short to reason.
- Fake words often have: unusual letter combos (*-gth* in odd places), real roots with wrong endings (*happiment*, *dangerful*), or near-misses of real words (*flurment*).
- Real words at high levels look rare but follow normal patterns: *ubiquitous, mitigate, cursory, pragmatic*.
- Duolingo's official tip: click **Yes** if you know it or think you've seen it before; click **No** if you haven't.
- Say the word silently in your head. Fakes often don't "sound" English.
- If you don't click within 5 seconds, it's marked wrong. Always click.

**How to train**
- Read the Academic Word List (570 families) and a news article daily.
- Learn common suffixes and which roots they attach to: *-tion, -ment, -ness, -ity, -ous, -ive, -able, -ize*.
- Practice with the official practice test and DET drill sites.

**Common fake-word traps**

| Looks real | Why it's fake |
|-----------|---------------|
| informate | real word is *inform* |
| knowledgement | real word is *acknowledgement* |
| succesful | misspelling (*successful*) |
| dangerful | wrong suffix (*dangerous*) |
| unpossible | wrong prefix (*impossible*) |

## 2. Fill in the Blanks

**What happens:** A sentence with one unfinished word (the second half is missing). Type the ending. 20 seconds. Scored right/wrong, and spelling must be correct. Missing words are nouns, verbs, adjectives or adverbs.

**Official tip:** look for another word in the sentence with the same or opposite meaning, or a common phrase the word belongs to.

**Strategy**
1. Read the full sentence.
2. Decide the part of speech (noun? verb? adjective?) and tense/number.
3. Use collocations: *heavy r___* → *rain*; *make a d___* → *decision*.
4. Check the letter count if blanks are shown.

**Example**
> Scientists have reached a general c______ that climate change is accelerating.
> → **consensus**

## 3. Read and Complete (C-test)

**What happens:** A ~100–150 word paragraph. Many words are missing their second half. Type the missing letters. 3 minutes total.

**Strategy**
1. Skim the whole passage first (15 s) for the topic.
2. Fill the easy words fast: *the, and, which, because*.
3. Return to harder words using context.
4. Check grammar endings: **-s, -ed, -ing, -ly**.
5. **Never leave a gap blank.** Each word is scored separately, so a guess can earn points.
6. Spelling must be correct to score.

**Official facts:** the title and first and last sentences are complete; unfinished words are never next to each other; no proper nouns or acronyms are cut. Texts can be fiction, news or textbook.

**Example**
> Bees play a cru___ role in agric______. Many cr___ depend on th___ for pollination.
> → crucial, agriculture, crops, them

**Training drill:** take any paragraph, cover the second half of every second word from sentence 2 onward, and complete it. 3 passages a day.

## 4. Interactive Reading

**What happens:** **Two passages per test**: one story (narrative) and one factual text (expository). One gets 7 minutes, the other 8. Each passage has 6 questions, always in this order:

| # | Sub-question | What you do | Scoring | Tip |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Complete the Sentences | Choose the right word for 5–10 gaps in the first half | Each word right/wrong | Easiest gaps first |
| 2 | Complete the Passage | Choose the missing sentence joining part 1 and part 2 | Right/wrong | Read part 2 carefully; it's new |
| 3–4 | Highlight the Answer (×2) | Click and drag the text that answers a question | Partial credit | Highlight only the answer, not the full sentence |
| 5 | Identify the Idea | Choose the main idea | Right/wrong | Not a small detail |
| 6 | Title the Passage | Choose the best title | Right/wrong | Use your Identify the Idea answer |

**Strategy**
- Each question reveals more of the text. Use earlier answers to help later ones.
- About 1 minute per question; watch the shared timer.
- Watch for distractors that are true but too narrow, or use words from the text with the wrong meaning.

## 02.01 The reading decision tree

Use this when a question type is not obvious or when you are losing time.

| If the task is... | First move | Then | Trap to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| One word, Yes/No | Say it silently | Decide on first instinct | Overthinking a plausible fake |
| One sentence, one broken word | Identify part of speech | Use collocation + meaning | Typing a different word form |
| A paragraph of half-words | Skim for topic | Fill easy words, then hard | Leaving gaps blank |
| Two texts, six questions each | Read the first half carefully | Use each question to reveal more | Spending 3 minutes on question 1 |
| Highlight the Answer | Read the question keywords | Drag only the answering phrase | Highlighting a whole paragraph |
| Identify the Idea | Ask "what is the whole text about?" | Eliminate detail-only options | Choosing a true but narrow detail |
| Title the Passage | Use your main-idea answer | Check the title covers the ending | Choosing a catchy but inaccurate title |

## 02.02 Timing plans

### Read and Select (15–18 words, 5 s each)

There is no thinking time. The only realistic plan:

- **0–2 s:** read the word.
- **2–4 s:** say it silently; check whether it "looks English".
- **4–5 s:** click. Never let the timer expire.

### Fill in the Blanks (6–9 items, 20 s each)

- **0–5 s:** read the whole sentence.
- **5–10 s:** decide part of speech and likely meaning.
- **10–15 s:** type the ending.
- **15–20 s:** reread for grammar and spelling.

### Read and Complete (3–6 paragraphs, 3 min each)

- **0–20 s:** skim the title and complete sentences.
- **20–90 s:** fill the easy gaps.
- **90–150 s:** use context for harder gaps.
- **150–180 s:** check endings, plurals, tense, spelling.

### Interactive Reading (2 passages, 7 and 8 min)

| Minute | Task |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | Read the first half; start Complete the Sentences |
| 1–3 | Finish Complete the Sentences (5–10 gaps) |
| 3–4 | Complete the Passage |
| 4–6 | Two Highlight the Answer questions |
| 6–7 | Identify the Idea + Title the Passage |
| 7–8 | Review flagged answers (only on the 8-minute passage) |

**Rule:** if a single gap takes more than 20 seconds, choose your best option and move on. One hard gap is not worth losing the main-idea questions.

## 02.03 Interactive Reading — full worked example

Read the passage and answer the six questions in order. Cover the answer key until you have finished.

> **The Last Train**
>
> Maya had planned to leave the laboratory by six, but the experiment refused to cooperate. At half past seven she was still adjusting the microscope, and the last train to Harrow left at eight fifteen. She packed her notebook into her bag, pulled on her coat, and hurried through the rain to the station.
>
> The platform was almost empty. A cleaner was mopping the far end, and a single vending machine hummed against the wall. Maya checked the departures board twice. The train was delayed by twelve minutes, which gave her time to buy a coffee and drink half of it before the announcement.
>
> When the train finally arrived, she chose a seat by the window and opened her notebook. The pages were full of numbers she had copied that afternoon, but one result still puzzled her. She had expected the reaction to slow down as the temperature rose; instead, it had become faster. She wrote a question in the margin and circled it.
>
> By the time the train reached Harrow, the rain had stopped. Maya walked home under a clear sky, thinking not about the delay but about the experiment. The answer, she suspected, was waiting for her in the morning.

### Question 1 — Complete the Sentences

Choose the correct word for each gap. The words in the passage have been removed.

> Maya had planned to leave the laboratory by six, but the experiment refused to (1) ______. At half past seven she was still adjusting the microscope, and the last train to Harrow left at eight fifteen. She packed her notebook into her bag, pulled on her coat, and (2) ______ through the rain to the station.
>
> The platform was almost (3) ______. A cleaner was mopping the far end, and a single vending machine hummed against the wall. Maya checked the departures board twice. The train was delayed by twelve minutes, which gave her just enough time to buy a coffee and drink (4) ______ of it before the announcement.
>
> When the train finally arrived, she chose a seat by the window and opened her notebook. The pages were full of numbers she had copied that afternoon, but one result still (5) ______ her.

**Options (one per gap):**

1. cooperate / collaborate / coordinate / connect
2. hurried / wandered / strolled / drifted
3. empty / quiet / silent / vacant
4. half / part / most / some
5. puzzled / confused / disturbed / interrupted

**Answers:** 1 cooperate · 2 hurried · 3 empty · 4 half · 5 puzzled

**Why:** "refused to cooperate" is a collocation; "hurried" matches the rush; "almost empty" describes a platform with few people; "drink half of it" is the natural quantity; "puzzled her" means confused her.

### Question 2 — Complete the Passage

Which sentence best joins the two parts?

> **Part 1:** … She wrote a question in the margin and circled it.
>
> **Part 2:** By the time the train reached Harrow, the rain had stopped. Maya walked home under a clear sky…

**Options:**
A. The train was now moving quickly through the countryside.
B. She closed the notebook and put it away for the rest of the journey.
C. Outside, the rain was still falling heavily against the window.
D. She had forgotten her umbrella at the laboratory.

**Answer:** B. It links the note-taking to the later walk home and explains why she is no longer writing. A is plausible but does not bridge the two parts; C contradicts "the rain had stopped"; D introduces an irrelevant detail.

### Question 3 — Highlight the Answer

**Question:** Why was Maya still in the laboratory at half past seven?

**Answer to highlight:** "the experiment refused to cooperate"

**Why:** The question asks for the cause of the delay, not for the time or the train schedule. Highlighting the whole first sentence would include unnecessary material.

### Question 4 — Highlight the Answer

**Question:** What surprised Maya about the result?

**Answer to highlight:** "She had expected the reaction to slow down as the temperature rose; instead, it had become faster."

**Why:** The surprise is the contrast between expectation and result. Highlighting only "it had become faster" is close but loses the contrast that explains the surprise.

### Question 5 — Identify the Idea

**Options:**
A. Maya missed the last train because of a delayed experiment.
B. A scientist's routine journey home is interrupted by bad weather.
C. A researcher spends a journey thinking about an unexpected result.
D. Trains are often delayed when the weather is bad.

**Answer:** C. The passage is about Maya's journey **and** her unresolved experimental result. A is factually wrong (she catches the train); B focuses on weather; D is a general detail not supported by the text.

### Question 6 — Title the Passage

**Options:**
A. A Delayed Train
B. The Last Train
C. Rain Over Harrow
D. Maya's Laboratory

**Answer:** B. "The Last Train" is the passage's stated setting and captures the journey framed by the experiment. A is accurate but narrow; C focuses on weather; D focuses on only one location.

## 02.04 Interactive Reading — question-by-question tactics

### Complete the Sentences

- The gaps test **collocation, grammar, and meaning**, not just synonyms.
- Read the whole sentence before looking at options. The correct word often depends on a word *after* the gap.
- Eliminate options that are grammatically impossible first.
- If two options seem possible, choose the one that fits the **tone** and the surrounding paragraph.

### Complete the Passage

- The missing sentence must connect **both** the sentence before and the sentence after.
- Look for pronouns and determiners: *this, these, such, another, however, therefore*.
- Reject options that introduce a new topic, contradict the passage, or repeat information already given.
- After choosing, read the join aloud in your head. Clunky joins are usually wrong.

### Highlight the Answer

- The answer is always **in the text**, but it may be paraphrased in the question.
- Match **meaning**, not identical words.
- Highlight the smallest phrase that fully answers the question.
- Do not highlight a full sentence unless the whole sentence is needed.
- If the question asks "why", look for a reason clause (*because, since, as, which led to*).

### Identify the Idea

- The main idea covers the **whole** passage, including the ending.
- Reject options that are:
  - true but only about one paragraph,
  - too broad (about a general topic rather than this text),
  - too narrow (a single detail),
  - contradicted by the ending.
- A good test: could the author summarize the passage with this sentence without leaving out a major part?

### Title the Passage

- Use your Identify the Idea answer as a bridge.
- A title should be accurate, concise, and cover the whole text.
- Beware of titles that are catchy but wrong, or that name only the setting.
- If two titles are accurate, choose the one that captures the **central conflict or idea**, not just the topic.

## 02.05 Distractor patterns to recognise

| Distractor type | How it tricks you | How to beat it |
|---|---|---|
| **True but narrow** | A detail that really appears in the text | Ask whether it covers the whole passage |
| **Word match** | Repeats vocabulary from the text but changes the meaning | Paraphrase the option back to yourself |
| **Opposite** | Uses the right topic but reverses the relationship | Check quantifiers and contrast words |
| **Out of scope** | Introduces a reasonable idea not in the passage | Point to the exact line that supports it |
| **Too strong** | Uses *all, never, always, only* | Check whether the text is that absolute |
| **Chronology trap** | Correct event, wrong order | Track time markers |
| **Pronoun trap** | Ambiguous *he/she/it/they* | Identify the noun the pronoun replaces |

## 02.06 Fill in the Blanks — suffix and grammar clues

The missing half is often decided by grammar, not vocabulary.

| Clue | What it tells you | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **a/an/the + ___ + noun** | Adjective | a *significant* increase |
| **subject + ___ + object** | Verb | researchers *conducted* a survey |
| **preposition + ___ + noun** | Noun | in *conclusion* |
| **more/most + ___ ** | Adjective | more *effective* |
| **has/have + ___ ** | Past participle | has *increased* |
| **to + ___ ** | Base verb | to *reduce* |
| **___ + -ly** | Adverb | *rapidly* changing |
| **plural noun needed** | -s/-es ending | several *reasons* |

**Common collocations that appear in Fill in the Blanks:**

- make a decision / an effort / progress
- take responsibility / action / a risk
- pay attention / a visit
- raise awareness / a question
- reach a conclusion / an agreement
- conduct research / a survey
- meet a deadline / requirements
- play a role / a part
- draw a conclusion / a distinction
- pose a threat / a question

## 02.07 Read and Complete — C-test survival rules

1. **The title and the first and last sentences are complete.** Use them to establish topic and tense.
2. **Unfinished words are never adjacent.** You always have a complete word between gaps.
3. **No proper nouns or acronyms are cut.** Do not waste time guessing a name.
4. **Each word is scored separately.** A blank is always worth a guess.
5. **Spelling must be exact.** If you are unsure between *-ance* and *-ence*, choose the more common form and move on.
6. **Use grammar first, meaning second.** Tense and plural endings are often enough to recover the word.
7. **Do not change the word's root.** If the visible part is *agric__*, the answer is *agriculture*, not *farming*.

**Recovery strategy when a gap is opaque:**

- Read the sentence before and after.
- Identify the part of speech.
- Say the sentence aloud with a placeholder ("The results were ___ enough to convince the committee").
- Try the most common academic words for that slot: *significant, sufficient, consistent, evident, considerable*.
- Move on and return if time remains.

## 02.09 Score killers in reading

- **Timing out on Read and Select.** An unanswered word is an automatic zero; a guess has a chance.
- **Leaving C-test gaps blank.** Partial credit is the whole point.
- **Highlighting too much.** Extra text lowers the Highlight score.
- **Choosing a detail as the main idea.** If it only describes one paragraph, it is wrong.
- **Ignoring the ending.** The last paragraph often resolves the main idea and eliminates a tempting title.
- **American vs British spelling panic.** Both are accepted in free writing; in Fill in the Blanks and Read and Complete, match the word exactly as the passage requires.

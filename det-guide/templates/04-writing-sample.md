# Template 04 — Writing Sample (5 minutes)

**Task:** One prompt, **30 seconds prep**, **5 minutes writing**. Aim for **140–180 words**; the official guide states no minimum.
**Audience:** Universities receive this unedited. Write as if admissions will read it.
**Goal:** 140–180 words, four-part short essay, accurate and specific.

## Step 1 — Decide the mode in 15 seconds

| Mode | Prompt signal | Opening move |
|---|---|---|
| **Argument** | agree/disagree, should, which is better | State your position |
| **Describe** | discuss, explain, what are the effects | Name two effects/features |
| **Recount** | describe a time, an experience, what you learned | Name the experience and its lesson |

## Step 2 — Plan three beats (15 seconds)

Write nothing down; keep it in your head.

1. **Position / focus:** ______________
2. **Point 1:** ______________
3. **Point 2:** ______________

## Step 3 — Four-part skeleton

```text
[TOPIC SENTENCE]
______________ (position/focus), for two main reasons.

[POINT 1 + DETAIL]
First, ______________. This is because ______________. For example, ______________.

[POINT 2 + DETAIL]
Second, ______________. As a result, ______________.

[CONCLUSION]
In conclusion, ______________.
```

## Mode-specific frames

### Argument

> I strongly believe that ______________ for two main reasons. First, ______________. For instance, ______________. Second, ______________. This matters because ______________. In conclusion, although some people may disagree, I remain convinced that ______________.

### Describe

> ______________ has two significant effects. The first is ______________. This can be seen in ______________. The second is ______________, which means that ______________. Overall, the implications of ______________ are far-reaching.

### Recount

> One experience that changed how I think about ______________ was ______________. At the time, ______________. I remember ______________. Since then, I have ______________. Looking back, the main lesson was ______________.

## Model (argument, 145 words)

> I believe that universities should require students to study at least one subject outside their main field, for two main reasons. First, interdisciplinary knowledge helps students solve complex problems. For example, a computer science student who studies psychology may design software that is easier for ordinary people to use. Second, studying a different subject develops intellectual flexibility. Students learn to approach problems from more than one angle, which is valuable in a rapidly changing job market. In conclusion, although specialisation is important, a broader education produces graduates who can adapt and communicate across disciplines.

## Time budget

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:30 | Read, choose mode, pick two points |
| 0:30–4:00 | Write 140–180 words |
| 4:00–4:40 | Add one example or repair a sentence |
| 4:40–5:00 | Proofread |

## University-reader rules

- Do not write something you would not want an admissions officer to read.
- Avoid slang, chat abbreviations, and jokes that depend on your friend group.
- Do not use a memorised essay. Answer the actual prompt.
- If you run out of ideas, add a **specific example**, not a repeated opinion.
- A clear, correct 130-word paragraph beats a confused 250-word one.

## Checklist

- [ ] I identified the mode correctly.
- [ ] My first sentence states the focus.
- [ ] I have **two** developed points.
- [ ] I included at least one **specific example**.
- [ ] My conclusion returns to the question.
- [ ] I wrote **140–180 words** (no official minimum is stated).
- [ ] I left time to proofread.
- [ ] The answer is appropriate for a university reader.

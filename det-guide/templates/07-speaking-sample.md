# Template 07 — Speaking Sample (3 minutes)

**Task:** One prompt, **30 seconds prep**, up to **3 minutes speaking**.
**Audience:** Universities receive this unedited. It is part of your application.
**Goal:** 2.5–3 minutes, structured, specific, and appropriate for admissions.

## Step 1 — Choose a structure

| Prompt type | Structure |
|---|---|
| Personal experience / "describe a time" | **PPF**: Present → Past → Future |
| Opinion / "do you agree" | **Point → Reason → Example → Conclusion** |
| Describe a person/place/object | **Overview → Detail 1 → Detail 2 → Significance** |

You do not need a conclusion that repeats everything. End with **why it matters**.

## Step 2 — PPF skeleton (most common)

```text
[PRESENT — 30–45 s]
I'd like to talk about ______________.
It is ______________. These days, ______________.

[PAST — 60–75 s]
I first ______________ when ______________.
I remember ______________. At the time, ______________.
What made it memorable was ______________.

[FUTURE — 45–60 s]
In the future, I hope to ______________ because ______________.
Looking back, ______________.

[CLOSE — 15–20 s]
That is why ______________ has been so important to me.
```

## Step 3 — Opinion skeleton

```text
[POSITION]
I believe that ______________ for two main reasons.

[REASON 1 + EXAMPLE]
First, ______________. For example, ______________.

[REASON 2 + EXAMPLE]
Second, ______________. This matters because ______________.

[COUNTERPOINT]
Some people might argue that ______________, but ______________.

[CONCLUSION]
Ultimately, I think ______________.
```

## Time budget

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:30 | Read; choose structure; pick two details |
| 0:30–0:50 | Opening (present/position) |
| 0:50–1:50 | Main detail/example 1 |
| 1:50–2:30 | Main detail/example 2 or future |
| 2:30–2:50 | Significance / conclusion |
| 2:50–3:00 | Stop cleanly; do not trail off |

## University-reader rules

- Choose a topic you would be comfortable discussing in an interview.
- Avoid politics, religion, graphic content, or anything you would not want a stranger to judge you on.
- Do not read from notes; you cannot use paper.
- Do not recite a memorised speech. It sounds flat and can be flagged.
- Specific detail beats generic praise. "The library had a huge map of the city on the wall" is better than "It was a great place."

## Model opening (personal experience)

> I'd like to talk about a journey that changed how I think about planning. Two years ago, I travelled to a small mountain town with two friends. We had a detailed itinerary, but almost nothing went according to plan. The bus broke down halfway there, we missed the last train, and we ended up staying in a tiny guesthouse owned by an elderly couple. At first, I was frustrated because I like to be in control. But that evening, the couple cooked dinner for us and told us stories about the town. Looking back, the unplanned part was the best part of the trip. In the future, I want to travel with more flexibility and say yes to unexpected detours. That experience taught me that plans are useful, but they should not stop you from enjoying what actually happens.

## If you run out of things to say

- Add a **sensory detail**: what you saw, heard, smelled, felt.
- Add a **contrast**: before vs after, expectation vs reality.
- Add a **lesson**: what you would do differently.
- Add a **future consequence**: how it affects your plans.

## Checklist

- [ ] I chose a clear structure in the first 30 seconds.
- [ ] I spoke for **2.5–3 minutes**.
- [ ] I used **specific details**, not general praise.
- [ ] I used a range of tenses.
- [ ] I included a lesson, outcome or significance.
- [ ] The content is appropriate for a university reader.
- [ ] I did not read or recite a memorised answer.

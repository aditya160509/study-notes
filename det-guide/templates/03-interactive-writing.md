# Template 03 — Interactive Writing (5 min + 3 min)

**Task:** Part 1 — **5 minutes** to answer a prompt. Part 2 — **3 minutes** to answer a follow-up. You **cannot edit part 1** during part 2.
**Goal:** One clear paragraph per part, 80–130 words in part 1, 50–80 in part 2.

## Step 1 — Identify the prompt type

| Type | Prompt looks like | Your job |
|---|---|---|
| **Argument** | "Do you agree or disagree?" / "Which do you recommend?" | Choose a side; give two reasons |
| **Describe** | "Discuss the implications/effects." | Explain two effects or features |
| **Recount** | "Describe a time…" / "What have you learned…" | Tell one specific experience |

Answering the wrong type loses **task relevance** marks.

## Step 2 — Part 1 skeleton (5 minutes)

```text
[TOPIC SENTENCE — opinion + controlling idea]
I ______________ (agree/disagree) with the idea that ______________ for two main reasons.

[REASON 1 + EXPLANATION]
Firstly, ______________. This is because ______________.

[EXAMPLE 1]
For instance, ______________.

[REASON 2 + EXPLANATION]
Another reason is that ______________. As a result, ______________.

[EXAMPLE 2 — optional if time]
For example, ______________.

[CONCLUSION — paraphrase, no new idea]
In conclusion, ______________.
```

## Step 3 — Part 2 skeleton (3 minutes)

The follow-up usually asks for an implication, solution, or personal reaction. Link it to part 1.

```text
[DIRECT ANSWER]
_____________ is important / problematic / useful because ______________.

[LINK TO PART 1]
As I mentioned earlier, ______________.

[DEVELOPMENT]
This means that ______________. For example, ______________.

[SHORT CONCLUSION]
Ultimately, ______________.
```

## Time budget

**Part 1**

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:30 | Read; choose side; pick two reasons |
| 0:30–3:45 | Write the paragraph |
| 3:45–4:30 | Add one example or fix a weak sentence |
| 4:30–5:00 | Proofread: articles, plurals, verb endings, punctuation |

**Part 2**

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | Read follow-up; decide direct answer |
| 0:15–2:30 | Write 3–5 sentences |
| 2:30–3:00 | Proofread |

## Phrase bank

**Opinion**
- I completely agree that …
- I strongly disagree with the idea that …
- In my view, … is the better option because …
- It could be argued that …, but I believe …

**Reasons**
- One main reason is that …
- This is largely because …
- Another consideration is …
- The main drawback/benefit is …

**Examples**
- For instance, …
- A clear example of this is …
- Personally, when I …, I found that …
- In my country, for example, …

**Results**
- As a result, …
- Consequently, …
- This means that …
- This can lead to …

**Conclusion**
- In conclusion, …
- For these reasons, …
- Overall, it seems clear that …

## Model part 1 (argument, 105 words)

> I completely agree with the idea that people are rarely satisfied with what they have, for two main reasons. Firstly, people tend to compare themselves with others. For instance, someone may have a good job and a comfortable home, but seeing friends with higher salaries can still make them feel dissatisfied. Another reason is that achieving one goal often creates a new, higher goal. Personally, after I bought my first laptop, I was happy for a few weeks, but I soon wanted a faster model. In conclusion, satisfaction fades quickly because people constantly redefine what "enough" means.

## Model part 2 (follow-up, 55 words)

> Advertising makes this tendency much stronger because it constantly shows people a better version of their lives. As I mentioned earlier, comparison is a major cause of dissatisfaction. Advertisements are designed to make us compare our possessions with idealised images, so they turn ordinary wants into perceived needs. Ultimately, this makes it harder to feel content with what we already have.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| No clear opinion in the first sentence | State your side immediately |
| Three reasons in 5 minutes | Use **two** developed reasons |
| No examples | Add one short personal or general example |
| Memorised essay that ignores the prompt | Mirror the prompt's exact topic |
| Editing part 1 during part 2 | You cannot; proofread part 1 before it closes |
| Using "I think" five times | Vary: *in my view, it seems, I believe, clearly* |

## Checklist

- [ ] My first sentence answers the question directly.
- [ ] I have a clear controlling idea ("for two main reasons").
- [ ] Each reason has an explanation or example.
- [ ] My conclusion paraphrases, not repeats word-for-word.
- [ ] Part 2 answers the follow-up and links to part 1.
- [ ] I used at least **four different linking words**.
- [ ] I proofread for articles, plurals, -s/-ed and spelling.
- [ ] I did not copy a memorised answer.

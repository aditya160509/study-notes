| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| Relative clause | Students **who manage their time well** usually perform better. |
| Second conditional | **If I had** more time, **I would** learn Japanese. |

| Passive voice | The bridge **was built** in 1990. |

## Most common errors to check

| Error | Wrong | Right |
|---|---|---|
| Articles | I am student | I am **a** student |
| Subject–verb | He go | He **goes** |
| Plural | many reason | many reason**s** |
| Tense | Yesterday I go | Yesterday I **went** |
| Uncountables | informations, advices | information, advice |
| Prepositions | depend of, interested about | depend **on**, interested **in** |
| Word form | It is a danger situation | It is a **dangerous** situation |
| Run-ons | I like it it is fun | I like it **because** it is fun |

## 06.01 Word families — learn the whole family, not one word

The DET tests **word forms** in Fill in the Blanks, Read and Complete, and writing. If you know *analyse* but not *analysis* or *analytical*, you will lose marks.

| Verb | Noun | Adjective | Adverb |
|---|---|---|---|
| analyse | analysis | analytical | analytically |
| benefit | benefit | beneficial | beneficially |
| conclude | conclusion | conclusive | conclusively |
| contribute | contribution | contributory | — |
| create | creation | creative | creatively |
| decide | decision | decisive | decisively |
| demonstrate | demonstration | demonstrative | demonstrably |
| differ | difference | different | differently |
| economy | economy | economic / economical | economically |
| emphasise | emphasis | emphatic | emphatically |
| evaluate | evaluation | evaluative | — |
| evolve | evolution | evolutionary | — |
| globe | globe | global | globally |
| innovate | innovation | innovative | innovatively |
| intend | intention | intentional | intentionally |
| interpret | interpretation | interpretive | — |
| maintain | maintenance | maintainable | — |
| perceive | perception | perceptive | perceptively |
| persist | persistence | persistent | persistently |
| predict | prediction | predictable | predictably |
| rely | reliance | reliable | reliably |
| signify | significance | significant | significantly |
| succeed | success | successful | successfully |
| vary | variation | variable / varied | variably |

**Common traps:**

- *economic* = about the economy; *economical* = cheap/efficient.
- *historic* = important in history; *historical* = relating to history.
- *sensitive* = responsive; *sensible* = reasonable.
- *imaginary* = not real; *imaginative* = creative.
- *considerable* = large; *considerate* = kind.

## 06.04 Grammar upgrade drills (with answers)

### Drill 1 — Articles

Correct the sentences.

1. I am student at university.
2. She is best candidate for job.
3. He gave me advice that was very useful.
4. We need information about the course.

**Answers:** 1 *I am **a** student at **a** university.* 2 *She is **the** best candidate for **the** job.* 3 *He gave me **advice** that was very useful.* (advice is uncountable) 4 *We need **information** about the course.*

### Drill 2 — Subject–verb agreement

1. The number of applicants have increased.
2. Each of the students have a laptop.
3. The research show a clear trend.
4. Neither the teacher nor the students was ready.

**Answers:** 1 *has* (the number… has). 2 *has* (each… has). 3 *shows* (research is singular). 4 *were* (nearest subject "students" is plural).

### Drill 3 — Tense

1. Yesterday I go to the library and study for three hours.
2. By the time we arrived, the film already started.
3. I am living here since 2020.
4. If I would have known, I would have helped.

**Answers:** 1 *went … studied*. 2 *had already started*. 3 *I have lived / I have been living here since 2020.* 4 *If I had known, I would have helped.*

### Drill 4 — Word form

1. The results were surprise.
2. She spoke with great confident.
3. It is a danger situation.
4. The economy growth was impressive.

**Answers:** 1 *surprising*. 2 *confidence*. 3 *dangerous*. 4 *economic*.

### Drill 5 — Sentence boundaries

1. I like the course it is very practical.
2. Although it was raining, we went out.
3. She studied hard, she passed the exam.
4. The report was long it was also unclear.

**Answers:** 1 *I like the course **because** it is very practical.* 2 correct. 3 *She studied hard**, so** she passed the exam.* (or full stop) 4 *The report was long**, and** it was also unclear.*

### Drill 6 — Relative clauses

Combine the sentences.

1. The student won the prize. She studied every day.
2. This is the book. It changed my thinking.
3. The professor gave feedback. Her lecture was excellent.

**Answers:** 1 *The student **who** studied every day won the prize.* 2 *This is the book **that/which** changed my thinking.* 3 *The professor **whose** lecture was excellent gave feedback.*

### Drill 7 — Conditionals

1. If I have more time, I will learn Japanese.
2. If I had more time, I would learn Japanese.
3. If I had had more time, I would have learned Japanese.

**Task:** explain the difference in meaning.

**Answer:** 1 real future possibility; 2 unreal present (I do not have time now); 3 unreal past (I did not have time then).

### Drill 8 — Passive

Rewrite in the passive.

1. Researchers conducted the survey in 2024.
2. The university will announce the results tomorrow.
3. Someone has removed the notice.

**Answers:** 1 *The survey **was conducted** by researchers in 2024.* 2 *The results **will be announced** tomorrow.* 3 *The notice **has been removed**.*

## 06.05 Paraphrase bank

The DET rewards paraphrase in reading, listening, and speaking. Practise saying the same idea in different words.

| Original | Paraphrase |
|---|---|
| The number increased sharply. | There was a steep rise. |
| The government should spend more. | Public spending ought to increase. |
| Many people believe… | It is widely thought that… |
| This is a serious problem. | This poses a significant challenge. |
| The results were not clear. | The findings were ambiguous. |
| It is important to act now. | Immediate action is essential. |
| The plan failed. | The initiative did not succeed. |
| People have different views. | Opinion is divided. |
| The cost went down. | Prices fell / declined. |
| The study found… | Research demonstrated… |
| Children should not use phones in class. | Phone use in lessons should be restricted. |
| Tourism can damage the environment. | Tourism can have a detrimental environmental impact. |

**Spelling rule reminders:**

- *i before e except after c* — but *receive*, *ceiling*; exceptions: *weird, seize*.
- Double the final consonant before -ed/-ing when the stress is on the last syllable: *begin → beginning*, *prefer → preferred*.
- Drop the final -e before -ing: *make → making*; keep it before -able: *manage → manageable*.
- Change -y to -i after a consonant: *happy → happiness*, *study → studied*.

## 06.07 Academic tone without sounding robotic

**Do:**

- Use precise verbs: *demonstrate, indicate, suggest, argue, propose*.
- Use hedges for claims: *tends to, appears to, may, is likely to*.
- Use impersonal framing where appropriate: *Research suggests…*, *It is widely accepted that…*
- Use concrete examples.

**Avoid:**

- *a lot of* → *many / numerous / a significant number of*
- *things* → *factors / issues / elements*
- *good/bad* → *beneficial/harmful*
- *get* → *obtain / receive / achieve*
- *big* → *substantial / significant*
- *nowadays* (overused) → *currently / in recent years*
- *I think* repeated → *I would argue / In my view / It seems to me*

**But do not overdo it.** *"The implementation of pedagogical interventions may facilitate the augmentation of academic outcomes"* is worse than *"Better teaching can improve results."* Clarity beats pretension.

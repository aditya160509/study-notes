# 03 — Listening

Listening tasks feed **Comprehension, Conversation, Literacy and Listening**.

---

## 1. Listen and Type

**What happens:** Hear one sentence. Type it exactly. Up to **3 plays**, ~1 minute.

**3-play method**
1. **Play 1:** just listen. Get the meaning. Type what you remember.
2. **Play 2:** fill the gaps.
3. **Play 3:** check *small words* — a/an/the, in/on/at, plurals, -ed endings.
4. **Leftover time:** check spelling **and punctuation** (capital letter, full stop, question mark). Scoring is partial credit: the closer to the exact sentence, the higher. It may be a sentence or a short paragraph.

**Where people lose points**
| Mistake | Example |
|---|---|
| Missing article | ~~She bought new car~~ → She bought **a** new car |
| Missing plural | ~~many student~~ → many student**s** |
| Missing -ed | ~~they walk home yesterday~~ → they walk**ed** |
| Contractions | Hear "they're" — write *they're*, not *their* |
| Homophones | there/their/they're, its/it's, to/too/two |

**Training:** dictation 10 sentences/day from podcasts (6 Minute English, TED-Ed). Compare with the transcript.

## 2. Interactive Listening (2 sets)

You play a university student. One set is a conversation between two students, the other is between a student and a professor. Topics: asking for help, giving advice, planning study, asking for information, making requests.

**Timing:** 6 min 30 s **shared** between Listen and Complete and Listen and Respond, then 75 s for the summary.

### a) Listen and Complete (new July 2025)
- You **hear** a short scenario and type the missing part of **3–4 sentences** about it.
- You can replay the scenario as many times as you want, but the clock is shared, so don't overdo it.
- **Spelling doesn't matter here; meaning does.** Write just enough to fill the gap, not too much.

### b) Listen and Respond (~5–6 turns)
- The speaker says something; you choose the **best next reply** from options.
- Correct answers are:
  - **On-topic** — respond to what was *just* said
  - **Right tone** — polite/formal with a professor, natural with a classmate
  - **Move the conversation forward**
- Official list of why answers are wrong: they don't fit the scenario, don't match what the other person said, use the wrong words or pronouns, are too formal or too informal, or add or leave out information.
- After each answer you see a green tick or red cross **and the correct line**. If you got one wrong, read the correct line; it helps you with the next turn.
- Sometimes you must choose how to start the conversation.
- Save a few seconds at the end to reread the conversation before the summary.

### c) Summarize the Conversation (75 seconds, written)
Write 3–4 complete sentences covering: **who + problem → advice/discussion → outcome**.

**Template**
> The student spoke with [the professor / a classmate] about [problem/topic]. [He/She] suggested [advice 1] and [advice 2]. They also discussed [detail]. In the end, the student decided to [outcome].

**Example**
> The student met with the professor because she was struggling to choose a topic for her research paper. The professor suggested narrowing her focus to local water pollution and using the university's database for sources. They also discussed the deadline for the first draft. In the end, the student agreed to send a short proposal by Friday.

**Tips**
- Scored on Writing criteria (content, coherence, grammar, vocabulary) and also counts toward Listening.
- Full sentences only, no bullet points. US or UK spelling are both fine. Reusing words from the conversation is fine.
- First person ("I asked my professor…") or third person ("Two students…") are both fine.
- Take mental notes on the problem, two pieces of advice, and the decision.
- Use past tense and reporting verbs: *suggested, recommended, explained, agreed, mentioned*.

## 03.01 Listening is a memory test under time pressure

The DET never asks you to analyse a lecture for 10 minutes. It asks you to hold **one short utterance** in working memory and reproduce or use it immediately. The skill to train is **chunking**, not note-taking volume.

**Three chunk types to listen for:**

| Chunk | Examples | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Content words** | *library, deadline, professor, extension* | Carry the meaning |
| **Function words** | *a, the, to, for, has, been* | Carry the grammar and the marks |
| **Intonation cues** | rising = question; stress = correction | Tell you what the speaker really means |

**Mental model:** listen for **who + action + detail + attitude**. Most DET listening items are built from those four slots.

---

## 03.02 Listen and Type — the 3-play method in detail

### Play 1 — meaning first

- Do **not** type while listening if typing distracts you. Listen to the whole sentence.
- Identify the sentence type: statement, question, request, opinion.
- Type the content words you remember, even if the grammar is incomplete.

### Play 2 — reconstruct

- Fill the function words: articles, prepositions, auxiliaries, conjunctions.
- Check verb endings: *-s, -ed, -ing*.
- Check plurals and possessives.
- If the speaker pauses, punctuation often follows: comma for a list or clause boundary, full stop at the end.

### Play 3 — verify

- Compare your sentence with the audio word by word.
- Focus on **small words** and **word order**.
- Check capitalization at the start and punctuation at the end.
- Do not rewrite the sentence in your own words. The scorer compares closeness to the original.

### Partial-credit strategy

If you cannot catch everything:

1. Get the **subject + verb + object** right.
2. Get **word order** right.
3. Add as many exact content words as you can.
4. Do not add words you did not hear — extra words can reduce the score.

### Common listening confusions

| Sound pair | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **-s / -es** | *walk* vs *walks* | Listen for a final hiss |
| **-ed** | *walk* vs *walked* | Listen for a final /t/ or /d/ |
| **can / can't** | *I can go* vs *I can't go* | "Can't" is longer and stressed |
| **thirteen / thirty** | *13* vs *30* | Stress on the second syllable = -teen |
| **fifteen / fifty** | *15* vs *50* | Same pattern |
| **he / she** | pronoun reference | Track the previous noun |
| **there / their / they're** | homophones | Use grammar: *their book*, *they're going* |
| **to / too / two** | homophones | *to* before a verb; *too* = also/excess; *two* = 2 |

### Dictation ladder (10 minutes a day)

| Level | Audio | Task |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6 Minute English, 30-second clip | Type 3 sentences exactly |
| 2 | TED-Ed, 45-second clip | Type 5 sentences, check articles |
| 3 | University lecture clip, 60 seconds | Type a 2-sentence summary + 3 exact sentences |
| 4 | DET-style item | One play only, then check |

**Correction rule:** compare against the transcript and mark every missing **article, preposition, plural, or ending** in a different colour. Those are your recurring losses.

## 03.03 Interactive Listening — how the set works

Each of the **2 scenarios** has three stages, always in this order:

1. **Listen and Complete** — 3–4 sentences with missing parts.
2. **Listen and Respond** — 5–6 turns; choose the best reply.
3. **Summarize the Conversation** — 75 seconds, written.

**Clock:** 6 min 30 s is shared by stages 1 and 2; the summary has its own 75 seconds.

**Scenario types:**

- student ↔ student: planning, advice, housing, societies, study groups.
- student ↔ professor: office hours, extensions, feedback, research topics, recommendations.

**Key advantage:** the two stages share a topic. What you learn in Listen and Complete helps you predict the replies and write a better summary.

---

## 03.04 Listen and Complete — tactics

- You can replay the scenario as often as you like, but the clock is shared. Cap yourself at **2–3 plays**.
- Read the three sentences **before** playing if the interface allows; predict what kind of information is missing (time? reason? object?).
- Type **only the missing idea**, not a full sentence.
- Spelling is not the priority; the idea is. If you cannot spell a word, use a simpler synonym you can spell.
- Keep answers short. "a fortnight" beats "a period of approximately fourteen days".
- If a gap asks for a number, write digits.
- If a gap asks for a reason, start with *because* only if the grammar of the sentence requires it.

**Example gap types:**

| Sentence | Missing part | Acceptable answer |
|---|---|---|
| The student needs an extension because ______. | reason | she was ill |
| The professor suggests meeting on ______. | time | Thursday afternoon |
| The main problem is that the ______ is due too soon. | object | assignment |
| They agree to ______ before the next class. | action | email the draft |

## 03.05 Listen and Respond — the tone and relevance filter

The correct reply must pass **four filters**:

1. **Relevance** — responds to what was just said, not to an earlier line.
2. **Tone** — formal/polite with a professor; relaxed but respectful with a classmate.
3. **Logic** — moves the conversation forward (accepts, declines, asks, clarifies, suggests).
4. **Grammar/reference** — pronouns and verb forms match the speaker.

**Wrong-answer patterns the official guide names:**

- does not fit the scenario,
- does not match what the other person said,
- uses the wrong words or pronouns,
- is too formal or too informal,
- adds or leaves out information.

**Practical method:**

- Read the speaker's line and silently paraphrase it: "She is asking for a deadline extension."
- Predict the function of the reply: accept, refuse, ask a question, give advice.
- Eliminate options with the wrong function first.
- Then eliminate options with the wrong tone.
- Choose the option that uses the same level of formality as the speaker.

**Useful academic reply functions:**

| Function | Professor | Classmate |
|---|---|---|
| Ask for clarification | "Could you explain what you mean by…?" | "Sorry, what do you mean by…?" |
| Ask for help | "I was wondering if you could help me with…" | "Could you give me a hand with…?" |
| Accept advice | "That sounds like a sensible approach." | "Yeah, that makes sense." |
| Decline politely | "I'm afraid I won't be able to…" | "I can't, unfortunately." |
| Negotiate a deadline | "Would it be possible to submit it by…?" | "Could we push it to…?" |
| Show understanding | "So, if I understand correctly, …" | "So you're saying…?" |

## 03.06 Full worked Interactive Listening set

**Scenario:** Office hours — a student asks a professor about a late assignment.

### Stage 1 — Listen and Complete

> **Audio (transcript):**
> Student: Professor Lane, do you have a minute? I'm worried about the research proposal.
> Professor: Of course. Come in. What's the issue?
> Student: I've been ill for the past week, and I haven't been able to finish the literature review. I was hoping to ask for an extension.
> Professor: I see. The proposal is due on Friday. How much extra time do you need?
> Student: Would it be possible to have until the following Wednesday?
> Professor: That should be fine, but you'll need to email me a short outline by Friday so I can see your progress.
> Student: Of course. I'll send that by Friday morning. Thank you.

**Complete the sentences:**

1. The student is worried about the ______.
2. The student has been ______ for the past week.
3. The professor asks for a short ______ by Friday.

**Answers:** 1 research proposal · 2 ill · 3 outline

**Note:** "literature review" is also in the audio, but sentence 1 asks what she is worried about overall — the proposal. Read the sentence, not just the audio.

### Stage 2 — Listen and Respond

**Turn 1 — Professor:** "Of course. Come in. What's the issue?"

A. "I'm fine, thanks. How are you?"
B. "I've been ill and I'm behind on the literature review."
C. "Can you write me a reference?"
D. "The library closes at nine."

**Answer:** B. A ignores the question; C jumps to a new request; D is unrelated.

**Turn 2 — Professor:** "The proposal is due on Friday. How much extra time do you need?"

A. "As much as possible, please."
B. "Would it be possible to have until the following Wednesday?"
C. "I don't know. What do you think?"
D. "Friday is fine."

**Answer:** B. It is specific, polite, and negotiable. A is vague; C pushes the decision back; D contradicts the request for an extension.

**Turn 3 — Professor:** "That should be fine, but you'll need to email me a short outline by Friday so I can see your progress."

A. "That's not fair. I'm ill."
B. "Of course. I'll send it by Friday morning."
C. "Can I send it next month?"
D. "I don't have an outline."

**Answer:** B. It accepts the condition and confirms a time. A is rude; C ignores the condition; D creates a new problem without solving it.

**Turn 4 — Professor:** "Good. And make sure you cite at least ten sources."

A. "Ten? That's impossible."
B. "Understood. I'll check my reference list."
C. "What's a source?"
D. "I'll cite them after the deadline."

**Answer:** B. It acknowledges the requirement and implies action. A is unhelpful; C is implausible at this level; D defers the requirement incorrectly.

### Stage 3 — Summarize the Conversation (75 seconds)

**Model answer:**

> The student met with Professor Lane because she had been ill and had fallen behind on the literature review for her research proposal. She asked for an extension beyond Friday, and the professor agreed to let her submit the proposal the following Wednesday. However, the professor asked her to email a short outline by Friday morning so that he could check her progress. The student agreed to send it and to make sure she cited at least ten sources.

**Why it scores:**

- Covers **who, problem, advice/decision, outcome**.
- Uses past tense and reporting verbs.
- Includes the condition (outline by Friday) — a detail many summaries miss.
- 4 complete sentences, no bullets.
- Accurate: does not invent a different deadline.

## 03.07 Summary templates by scenario

### Student ↔ professor: extension

> The student spoke with [professor] because [problem]. [He/She] asked for [request]. The professor agreed to [outcome], but asked the student to [condition]. The student promised to [action].

### Student ↔ professor: feedback

> The student met with [professor] to discuss [assignment]. The professor said that [strength] but suggested [improvement]. They also discussed [detail]. The student plans to [next step].

### Student ↔ classmate: planning

> Two students discussed [task]. One suggested [idea], and the other agreed/disagreed because [reason]. They decided to [outcome] and agreed to [next action].

### Student ↔ classmate: problem

> One student explained that [problem]. The other recommended [advice 1] and [advice 2]. In the end, they agreed to [solution].

### Student ↔ professor: request

> The student asked the professor for [request]. The professor explained [condition/rule] and suggested [alternative]. The student agreed to [action].

## 03.08 What loses marks in the summary

| Mistake | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Missing the outcome | Describes the problem but not the decision | End with "In the end, …" |
| Wrong tense | "She asks for an extension" | Use past tense: "She asked" |
| Bullet points | Lists three fragments | Full sentences only |
| Invented detail | Adds a deadline not in the audio | Use only what you heard |
| Too long | 8 sentences, no time to proofread | 3–4 sentences |
| No condition | Omits "email an outline by Friday" | Include the professor's requirement |
| First-person confusion | "I asked my professor" when you are summarising | First or third person is fine, but be consistent |

## 03.09 Listening traps that repeat

### Paraphrase traps

The correct answer rarely repeats the speaker's exact words. Learn these mappings:

| Audio says | Answer says |
|---|---|
| "I haven't been able to finish" | "The student is behind" |
| "Would it be possible to…" | "The student requests…" |
| "That should be fine" | "The professor agrees" |
| "You'll need to…" | "The professor requires…" |
| "I'm afraid I can't…" | "The student declines" |
| "Let's meet halfway" | "They compromise" |

### Number and date traps

- *the following Wednesday* = the Wednesday after the current week, not tomorrow.
- *by Friday* means **before** Friday, not on Friday.
- *a fortnight* = two weeks.
- *the end of the month* is not a specific date.
- Ordinals: *first, second, third*; dates: *the 3rd of May*.

### Attitude traps

Listen for tone words:

- positive: *That's great, perfect, exactly, helpful*
- negative: *I'm afraid, unfortunately, not quite, that's a problem*
- hesitant: *I'm not sure, maybe, it depends*

The reply must match the attitude, not just the topic.

## 03.11 Score killers in listening

- **Typing during the first play** and missing the meaning.
- **Ignoring articles and plurals** in Listen and Type.
- **Choosing a reply that answers the previous line**, not the current one.
- **Being too casual with a professor** or too formal with a classmate.
- **Writing a summary that is a list of facts** rather than who–problem–outcome.
- **Spending all 6:30 on Listen and Complete**, leaving no time to reread before the summary.
- **Adding information you did not hear** because it seems logical.

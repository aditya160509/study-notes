## The 10 rules that matter most

1. The test is **adaptive**. Harder questions mean you're doing well. Don't panic.
2. **Never leave a gap blank.** A guess can score; an empty gap can't.
3. **Use the full time** on writing and speaking. More relevant language = more evidence of your level.
4. **Range wins:** varied vocabulary, linkers, complex sentences.
5. **Proofread** every written answer in the last 10–15 seconds.
6. **Small words cost points** in Listen and Type: articles, prepositions, plural -s, -ed endings.
7. **Speak steadily**, not fast. A short pause beats "um".
8. **Memorise structures, never full answers.** Memorised or off-topic answers get flagged.
9. The **Writing and Speaking Samples go to universities** unedited. Treat them like part of your application.
10. Take the **free official practice test** every week and track your weakest subscore.

## A realistic 7-day route

1. **Day 1:** free practice test; read file 01; note your two weakest tasks.
2. **Day 2:** reading drills (file 02) + 50 vocabulary cards.
3. **Day 3:** listening drills (file 03) + one summary.
4. **Day 4:** writing (file 04): 3 photos + 1 Interactive Writing.
5. **Day 5:** speaking (file 05): record 6 answers + 1 long answer.
6. **Day 6:** full mock; tech rehearsal with phone camera and room scan.
7. **Day 7:** review error log; light practice; sleep.

**Do not** learn a new grammar topic in the last 48 hours. Fix timing, setup, and your two weakest task types instead.

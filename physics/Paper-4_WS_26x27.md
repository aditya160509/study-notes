#### Topic 12: Motion in a Circle

#### Define

- Define the radian → The **angle subtended at the centre of a circle** when the **arc length is equal to** **the radius**.
#### State

- State the names of two quantities that vary during the motion of an object in a circle at constant speed → **Velocity**, **momentum**, **centripetal acceleration**, or **resultant force**.
- State the relationship between degrees and radians → **2π rad = 360°**.
- State the relationship between linear speed *v* and angular speed *ω* → **v = rω**.
- State the significance of the direction of the centripetal force → A force of constant magnitude that is **always perpendicular to the direction of** **motion**.
#### Explain

- Explain why an object moving in a circle at constant speed is accelerating → Velocity is a vector; the **direction of motion is constantly changing**, therefore velocity changes, which is acceleration.
- Explain why the radius of the circular path of a ball in a hollow cone increases when its speed is increased → The resultant force (and centripetal acceleration) is fixed by the **weight** and **cone** **angle**; since **a = v² / r**, if **speed increases**, the **radius must increase** to maintain constant acceleration.
- Explain the effect on the angular speed of bicycle pedals if the chain is moved to a smaller rear cog at constant bicycle speed → The chain moves through a **smaller distance per wheel rotation**; the **linear** **speed of the chain decreases**, so the **angular speed of the pedals decreases**.
- Explain why a particle follows a circular path in a uniform magnetic field → The magnetic **force is always perpendicular to the velocity**; this constant- magnitude force provides the **centripetal force**.
#### Describe

- Describe how the forces acting on a ball in a hollow cone result in centripetal acceleration → The **vertical component** of the normal contact force **balances the weight**; the **horizontal component** provides the **resultant force towards the centre** of the circle.
#### Show (Theoretic Derivations)

- Show that the angular speed *ω* is related to the period *T* → **ω = 2π / T**.
- Show that the centripetal acceleration *a* can be expressed in terms of *v* and *r* → **a = v² / r**.

- Show that the centripetal acceleration *a* can be expressed in terms of *r* and *ω* → **a = rω²**.
- Show that the centripetal force *F* can be expressed as → **F = mrω²** or **F = mv² / r**.

#### Topic 15: Ideal Gases

#### Define

- Define the Avogadro constant → The **number of atoms in 12 g of carbon-12**.
- Define the mole → The **amount of substance** containing the same **number of particles** as **atoms** **in 12 g of carbon-12**.
- Define the Boltzmann constant → The **molar gas constant divided by the Avogadro constant** (**R / Nₐ**).
#### State

- State what is meant by an ideal gas → A gas that obeys **pV proportional to T** (or **pV = nRT**), where **T is the** **thermodynamic temperature**.
- State the basic assumptions of the kinetic theory of gases → Molecules are in **constant random motion**; collisions are **perfectly elastic**; there are **no intermolecular forces** (except during collisions); the **volume of molecules** **is negligible**; collisions are **instantaneous**.
- State the value of absolute zero → **0 K** or **−273.15 °C**.
- State what is represented by the symbol ⟨c²⟩ → The **mean-square speed** of the molecules.
#### Explain

- Explain qualitatively how molecular movement causes gas pressure → Collisions with container walls cause a **change in momentum**; the **force is the** **rate of change of momentum**; pressure is the **sum of forces per unit area**.
- Explain why the internal energy of an ideal gas is solely kinetic → There are **no intermolecular forces**, so the **potential energy is zero**.
- Explain why the internal energy of an ideal gas is proportional to temperature → With **zero potential energy**, internal energy equals **total kinetic energy**, which is **proportional to thermodynamic temperature**.
- Explain the effect of changing gas density at constant temperature on the r.m.s. speed → **No effect**; r.m.s. speed **depends only on thermodynamic temperature**.
- Explain why some helium atoms escape the atmosphere even if the temperature is below the required threshold → Molecular speeds follow a **distribution**; some molecules have speeds **greater** **than the escape speed**, even if the **average (r.m.s.) speed** is lower.

- Show that the mean density of a planet is related to orbital period **T** and the ratio **n** of orbital radius to planet radius → Equate **gravitational force** to **centripetal force**; substitute **mass in terms of**

**density and volume**; express **angular velocity in terms of period** to obtain → **ρ = 3πn³ / GT²**.

#### Topic 16: Thermodynamics

#### Define

- Define internal energy → The **sum of a random distribution of kinetic and potential energies** associated with the **molecules of a system**.
- Define the term **+ ΔU** → The **increase in the internal energy** of the system.
- Define the term **+ q** → The **thermal energy supplied to the system** (or **heating of the system**).
- Define the term **+ w** → The **work done on the system**.
#### State

- State the first law of thermodynamics → **ΔU = q + w**, where **ΔU** is the **increase in internal energy**, **q** is the **heating of the** **system**, and **w** is the **work done on the system**.
- State what is represented by a negative value of **ΔU** → A **decrease in internal energy**.
- State the formula for work done on a gas at constant pressure → **w = −pΔV**, where **pΔV** is the **work done by the gas**.
- State the relationship between temperature and internal energy → A **rise in temperature** of an object results in an **increase in its internal energy**.
#### Explain

- Explain why the internal energy of an ideal gas is solely kinetic → There are **no intermolecular forces** in an ideal gas; therefore, the **potential** **energy of the molecules is zero**.
- Explain the change in internal energy during melting at constant temperature → The **potential energy** of molecules increases as **bonds are broken or** **weakened**; the **kinetic energy remains constant** due to constant temperature; therefore, **internal energy increases**.
- Explain the sign of work done during gas expansion → As the gas expands, it does work against the surroundings; therefore, the **work** **done on the gas is negative**.
- Explain the sign of work done during gas compression → As the gas is compressed, the surroundings do work on it; therefore, the **work** **done on the gas is positive**.
- Explain internal energy changes in a complete cycle (closed loop) → The **total change in internal energy is zero** because the system returns to its **original state and temperature**.
- Explain why internal energy is determined by the state of the system → Internal energy depends only on the **current conditions** (such as **temperature** and **volume/pressure**) and not on the **process or path** taken.
- Explain the energy transfers in a cycle where a gas does net work → If the gas does a **net amount of work** over a cycle, there must be a **net input of** **thermal energy** so that the **overall change in internal energy remains zero**.

- Explain the internal energy of a solid ball falling freely in a vacuum → The **random kinetic and potential energies** of the molecules do not change; there is **no heating** and **no work done on the ball**; therefore, **internal energy** **remains unchanged**.
- Explain why specific heat capacity at constant volume is less than at constant pressure → At **constant volume**, **no work is done** (**w = 0**), so all supplied thermal energy increases **internal energy**; at **constant pressure**, the gas expands and **does work**, so **more thermal energy** is required for the same temperature rise.
#### Show (Theoretic Derivations)

- Show how to calculate work done from a **p–V graph** → Work done is equal to the **area under the line or curve** on the graph.
- Show that **ΔU = q + w** for an adiabatic process → In an adiabatic change, **no thermal energy is transferred** (**q = 0**); therefore, **ΔU** **= w**.
- Show the relationship between internal energy and **pV** for an ideal gas → The internal energy **U** of an ideal gas is **directly proportional to the product pV**.

#### Topic 17: Oscillations

#### Define

- Define simple harmonic motion (SHM) → Motion where **acceleration is proportional to displacement** from a fixed point and is **always in the opposite direction** to the displacement.
- Define angular frequency → The product of **(2** π**)** and the **frequency**, or equivalently **(2** π**) divided by the** **period**.
- Define resonance → **Maximum amplitude** of oscillations when the **forced (driving) frequency equals** **the natural frequency** of the system.
- Define damping → The **loss of energy** from an oscillating system due to **resistive forces** (such as friction or air resistance).
#### State

- State the significance of the minus (–) sign in the SHM equation (a = - ω² x) → It shows that **acceleration and displacement are in opposite directions**, meaning acceleration is always directed towards the **equilibrium position**.
- State what is meant by the natural frequency of a system → The frequency at which a system **oscillates when free to do so**, without external driving or resistive forces.
- State the condition for an object to lose contact with an oscillating platform → Contact is lost when the **downward acceleration** of the platform **exceeds the** **acceleration due to gravity** (g).
- State the energy represented by the different lines in an SHM energy–displacement graph → A **horizontal line** represents **total energy**; a **U-shaped curve** represents **potential energy**; an **inverted U-shaped curve** represents **kinetic energy**.
#### Explain

- Explain how a displacement–acceleration graph shows SHM → A **straight line through the origin** shows acceleration is proportional to displacement; a **negative gradient** shows acceleration acts in the opposite direction to displacement.
- Explain why the amplitude of oscillations decreases in a damped system → The system **loses energy** to the surroundings because it must **do work against** **resistive forces**, such as viscous drag.
- Explain the interchange of kinetic and potential energy during SHM → **Kinetic energy is maximum at the equilibrium position** and zero at maximum displacement; **potential energy is maximum at maximum displacement** and zero at equilibrium; the **total energy remains constant** in the absence of damping.
- Explain why a metal block oscillating between magnets is damped → The block **cuts magnetic flux**, inducing an **e.m.f.**; this causes **eddy currents** to flow, which **dissipate thermal energy**; this energy loss comes from the **mechanical** **energy of the oscillations**.

#### Describe

- Describe the difference between light, critical, and heavy damping → **Light damping**: oscillations continue with **gradually decreasing amplitude**; → **Critical damping**: the system returns to equilibrium in the **shortest possible time** without overshooting; → **Heavy damping**: the system returns to equilibrium **slowly** without oscillating.
- Describe the use of a fiducial mark → A mark placed at the **equilibrium position** to assist in **counting oscillations** and improving the **accuracy of timing**.

#### Topic 20: Magnetic Fields

#### Definitions and Units

- Define magnetic field → A **region** of space where a **force** is exerted on a **magnetic pole**, a **moving** **charge**, or a **current-carrying conductor**.
- Define magnetic flux density → The **force** acting **per unit current per unit length** on a straight wire placed **perpendicular** to the magnetic field.
- Define the tesla → The magnetic flux density that produces a **force of 1 newton per metre** on a conductor carrying a **current of 1 ampere** placed **perpendicular** to the field.
- Define magnetic flux → The **product** of the **magnetic flux density** and the **area perpendicular** to the direction of the magnetic flux density.
- Define magnetic flux linkage → The **product** of the **magnetic flux** and the **number of turns** on the coil.
#### Forces on Charged Particles

- Explain why a charged particle follows a circular path in a uniform magnetic field → The magnetic **force** is always **perpendicular** to the **velocity**; this force has a **constant magnitude** and acts as the **centripetal force**.
- Explain why the speed of a particle is not affected by a magnetic field → The magnetic **force** is **perpendicular** to the **direction of motion**; therefore **no** **work is done** by the force on the particle.
- State the condition for a charged particle to experience no magnetic force → The particle must be **stationary** or moving **parallel** to the **magnetic field**.
- Describe the motion of a particle entering a field at an angle (non-perpendicular) → The component of velocity **parallel** to the field remains constant while the **perpendicular** component causes circular motion, resulting in a **helical** path.
#### The Hall Effect

- Explain the origin of the Hall voltage → The magnetic field exerts a **force** on **moving charge carriers**; charges are **deflected** to opposite faces of the conductor; an **electric field** is created which opposes the magnetic force; accumulation stops when the **electric force equals the** **magnetic force**.
- State the meaning of (n) in the Hall voltage formula → The **number density** of **charge carriers** (number of free electrons per unit volume).
- Suggest why a Hall probe is made of a semiconductor rather than a metal → Semiconductors have a **much smaller value** of **number density** (n); since (V_H) is **inversely proportional** to (n), the Hall voltage is **larger** and easier to measure.
- Explain why the current in a Hall probe must be constant → The Hall voltage is **proportional** to the **current**; a constant current ensures (V_H) depends only on the **magnetic flux density**.

#### Magnetic Fields due to Currents

- Describe the magnetic field around a long straight wire → **Concentric circles** centered on the wire, with **spacing increasing** as distance from the wire increases.
- Describe the magnetic field inside a long solenoid → The field is **uniform**, represented by **straight**, **parallel** lines along the axis.
- Explain the origin of the forces between two parallel current-carrying conductors → Each wire lies in the **magnetic field** produced by the other; since the **current** is **perpendicular** to that field, each wire experiences a **magnetic force**.
- State the relationship between the forces on two parallel wires with different currents → The forces are **equal in magnitude** and **opposite in direction** due to **Newton’s** **third law** (or because force is proportional to the **product of both currents**).
#### Electromagnetic Induction

- State Faraday’s law of electromagnetic induction → The **induced e.m.f.** is **directly proportional** to the **rate of change** of **magnetic** **flux linkage**.
- State Lenz’s law → The direction of the induced e.m.f. is such as to **oppose the change** that caused it.
- Explain why an e.m.f. is induced in a coil near a solenoid when current is switched on → The increasing current creates a **changing magnetic flux**; this changing flux **links** the coil, inducing an **e.m.f.**.
- Explain the use of an iron core in a transformer → To **concentrate** the magnetic flux and **increase the flux linkage** between the primary and secondary coils.
- Explain why a transformer core is laminated → To **reduce eddy currents** in the core, thereby **minimising energy losses** due to thermal dissipation.
- Explain electromagnetic damping → Oscillations cause a **change in flux linkage**; an **e.m.f.** and **current** are induced; the current **dissipates thermal energy**; this energy is taken from the **kinetic/potential energy of the oscillations**.
- Explain the principle of a velocity selector → **Electric and magnetic fields** are applied at **right angles**; particles experience **equal and opposite forces**; only particles with a **specific velocity** (v = E/B) pass through **undeviated**.

#### Topic 21: Alternating Currents

#### Definitions and Characteristics

- **Define root-mean-square (r.m.s.) current** → The value of **direct current** that produces the **same heating effect** (or dissipates the same mean power) as the alternating current in a given resistor.
- **Define peak value** → The **maximum value** (voltage or current) of a sinusoidal waveform in either direction from the zero position.
#### Power in A.C. Circuits

- **Explain why the mean power is half the maximum power for a sinusoidal a.c.** → Heating effect depends on **( I² )**; the square of a sine wave is **always positive**, and its average value over a cycle is **half of its peak value**.
- **Explain why the mean current of a sinusoidal a.c. is zero** → The current flows in **opposite directions** for equal times during each half-cycle, so the **net charge flow** over a complete period is zero.
- **Explain the advantage of high-voltage a.c. transmission** → For a given power, a higher voltage results in a **lower current**; a lower current **reduces power losses** ( I²R ) in the transmission cables.
#### Rectification and Smoothing

- **Define rectification** → The process of **converting alternating current (a.c.) into direct current (d.c.)**.
- **Distinguish between half-wave and full-wave rectification** → **Half-wave:** Only **one half** of the a.c. cycle is allowed to pass to the output. → **Full-wave:** Both halves of the cycle are redirected to flow in the **same direction** through the load.
- **Explain the use of a bridge rectifier** → Uses **four diodes** arranged so that the current always flows in the **same** **direction through the load**, regardless of the input polarity.
- **Define smoothing** → The **reduction in the variation** (ripple) of the output voltage or current.
- **Explain how a capacitor produces smoothing** → The capacitor **charges up** to the peak voltage and then **discharges slowly** through the load resistor when the input voltage drops, maintaining the output voltage.
- **Explain the effect of capacitance and load resistance on smoothing** → Increasing the **capacitance** or the **load resistance** increases the **time constant** ( RC ), resulting in a **slower discharge** and **less ripple**.

- **Explain why emitted electrons have a range of kinetic energies** → The work function is the **minimum** energy to remove an electron; electrons originating **below the surface** require additional energy to escape, leaving them with **less kinetic energy** on emission.

- **Describe the formation of an emission line spectrum** → Excited electrons **de-excite to lower energy levels**, emitting photons with energy equal to the **energy level difference** ( hf = E₁ - E₂ ).
- **Describe the formation of an absorption line spectrum** → Electrons **absorb photons** of specific energies from a continuous spectrum and are **excited to higher levels**; re-emission occurs in all directions, producing **dark** **lines** in the transmitted spectrum.

#### Topic 23: Nuclear Physics

#### I. Definitions and Core Concepts

1. **Mass Defect** o The **difference** between the **mass of a nucleus** and the **sum of the masses**
**of its individual nucleons** when they are **infinitely separated**.

2. **Nuclear Binding Energy** o The **minimum energy** required to **separate all nucleons** in a nucleus to
**infinite separation**.

3. **Binding Energy per Nucleon** o The **average energy per nucleon** required to remove a nucleon from the
nucleus; o It is a **measure of nuclear stability**.

4. **Radioactive Decay Constant (\lambda)** o The **probability per unit time** that a given nucleus will decay.
5. **Activity (A)** o The **number of nuclear disintegrations per unit time**, related to the
number of undecayed nuclei by [A = -λ N]

6. **Half-life (t1/2)** o The **time taken** for the **activity** or the **number of undecayed nuclei** in a
sample to fall to **half its initial value**.

#### II. Characteristics of Radioactive Decay

1. **Spontaneous Nature** o Radioactive decay is **unaffected by external conditions**, including
**temperature**, **pressure**, or **chemical state**.

2. **Random Nature** o It is **impossible to predict** which nucleus will decay or when; however, each
nucleus has a **constant probability of decay per unit time**. o This randomness is evidenced by **statistical fluctuations** in measured count rate.

3. **Exponential Decay Law** o The decay of radioactive quantities follows
[x = x₀ e {-λ t}] where (x) may represent **activity (A)**, **number of undecayed** **nuclei (N)**, or **count rate**.

#### III. Nuclear Reactions: Fission and Fusion

1. **Nuclear Fusion** o The process in which **two light nuclei combine** to form a **heavier nucleus**. o Requires **very high temperatures** to provide sufficient kinetic energy to
overcome **electrostatic repulsion** between nuclei.

2. **Nuclear Fission** o The splitting of a **heavy nucleus** into **two or more lighter nuclei** of roughly
comparable mass.

3. **Energy Release in Nuclear Reactions** o Energy is released because the **total binding energy of the products is**
**greater** than that of the reactants.

o Equivalently, the **total mass of the products is less** than that of the reactants, with the mass difference converted to energy via [E = mc²]

4. **Binding Energy per Nucleon Curve** o When plotted against nucleon number (A):
- Steep rise for **light nuclei**
- Maximum at approximately **(56) (iron)**
- Gradual decrease for **heavy nuclei**
o Explains why **fusion** is favourable for light nuclei and **fission** for heavy nuclei.

#### IV. Radiation and Decay Particles

1. **Alpha Particles** o Emitted with **discrete energies** because the decay energy is fixed and
shared only between the **alpha-particle** and the **recoiling daughter** **nucleus**.

2. **Beta Particles** o Emitted with a **continuous spectrum of energies** because the decay energy
is shared among **three particles**:

- the beta-particle,
- the daughter nucleus,
- and an **anti-neutrino**.
3. **Annihilation** o Occurs when a particle meets its **antiparticle** (e.g. electron–positron). o Their **entire rest mass** is converted into energy, typically producing **two**
**gamma photons** emitted in **opposite directions** to conserve momentum.

4. **Activity vs Count Rate** o The **measured count rate** is always **less than the activity** because:
- radiation is emitted in **all directions**,
- some radiation is **absorbed** before detection,
- detectors are **not 100% efficient**.

#### Topic 24: Medical Physics

#### Production and Use of Ultrasound

Ultrasound imaging relies on the **piezo-electric effect**. A **piezo-electric crystal** changes shape when a potential difference (p.d.) is applied across it and, conversely, generates an

e.m.f. when its shape is changed by external pressure.
- **Generation and Detection:** In a transducer, an alternating p.d. makes the crystal vibrate at its natural frequency to emit ultrasound. For detection, reflected ultrasound pulses cause the crystal to vibrate, which generates a measurable e.m.f..
- **Specific Acoustic Impedance ($Z$):** This is defined as the **product of the density** **of a medium and the speed of sound** in that medium
- **Reflection Coefficient:** When ultrasound hits a boundary between two tissues, the intensity reflection coefficient depends on the difference between their acoustic impedances. If Z₁and Z₂ are very different (such as at an air-tissue boundary), **almost all the ultrasound is reflected**.
- **Coupling Gel:** To ensure ultrasound enters the body, a gel with an impedance similar to skin is used to **minimise reflection at the air-skin interface**.
#### Production and Use of X-rays

X-rays are produced when a **metal target is bombarded by high-speed electrons**.

- **Wavelength and Attenuation:** The **minimum wavelength** is determined by the accelerating potential difference applied to the electrons. As X-rays pass through matter, their intensity decreases exponentially μ is the **linear attenuation** **coefficient**.
- **Contrast:** This refers to the **difference in the degree of blackening** between various structures in an image. High contrast is achieved when tissues (like bone and muscle) have significantly different attenuation coefficients.
#### Computed Tomography (CT) Scanning

While standard X-rays provide a 2D "shadow" image, a **CT scan produces a 3D image** of internal structures.

- **Process:** Multiple X-ray images are taken of a single section (or "slice") from **different angles** to build a 2D image of that section.
- **3D Reconstruction:** This process is repeated for many sections along an axis, and the 2D images are combined using a computer to build a **three-dimensional** **representation**.
#### PET Scanning

Positron Emission Tomography (PET) uses a **radioactive tracer**—a substance containing nuclei that undergo **$\beta^+$ decay**.

- **Annihilation:** Positrons emitted by the tracer interact with electrons in the body tissue. This results in **annihilation**, where the mass of the electron-positron pair is converted entirely into energy.
- **Gamma Photons:** The annihilation produces a **pair of gamma-ray photons** that travel in **opposite directions** to conserve momentum.
- **Imaging:** Detectors outside the body pick up these photons; by processing the **difference in arrival times**, the location of the annihilation and the concentration of the tracer in the tissue can be mapped.
